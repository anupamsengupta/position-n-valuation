# EU Power — Instrument Calculation Requirements

**Clustered by instrument type for functional specification generation.**  
Each section is self-contained and can be fed independently to a spec generation agent.  
Version 1.1 — May 2026

**Target platform:** Multitenant SaaS CTRM/ETRM for 150–200 small-to-mid EU energy trading firms.  
**Stack:** Java 21, Spring Boot 3.3, Aurora PostgreSQL 16, Kafka 3.7, Redis 7.  
**Markets:** EPEX, EEX, NORDPOOL, XBID. Bidding zones: DE-LU, AT, FR, NL, BE, NO1-5, SE1-4, DK1-2, FI.  
**Settlement granularity:** 15-min (DE, AT, NL, BE, FR), hourly (Nordics, Iberia).

**Document usage:** Each instrument section (1–7) is designed to be passed independently to a functional specification generation agent. The agent should produce FR-nnn functional rules, data dependencies, validation constraints, and error handling from each requirement. Section 8 (portfolio-level) depends on outputs from all instrument sections. Section 9 defines phasing.

## 1. Day-Ahead (DA) — Exchange Spot

**Exchange:** EPEX Spot day-ahead auction.  
**Granularity:** Hourly (all zones) + 15-min (DE, AT, NL, BE, FR).  
**Settlement:** D+2 cash settlement via exchange clearing (ECC).  
**Delivery:** Physical, next calendar day.  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 50–200 per day (one per delivery interval traded).  
**Position lifecycle:** Trade → nominate → deliver → settle. No forward valuation — fully realized within 48 hours.

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| DA-VOL-01 | Auction volume resolution | Volume per hourly or 15-min contract as executed in the DA auction. Fixed at auction close (12:00 CET D-1). No shaping needed — volume is at native market granularity. The system must accept DA results as a batch feed from the exchange (typically via FTP or API), parse the execution report, and create one deal per executed contract with the exact volume and delivery interval. If the tenant participates in multiple bidding zones, each zone's auction produces separate results. The system must validate that the sum of interval volumes equals the total MWh reported by the exchange for the auction session. |
| DA-VOL-02 | Block order decomposition | Block orders (baseload, peak, off-peak, custom blocks) must be decomposed into their constituent hourly/15-min intervals for position aggregation. Block MW applies uniformly to all intervals in the block definition. For example, a baseload block of 20 MW for delivery day 15-Mar covers all 24 hours (or 96 quarter-hours) at 20 MW each. A peak block covers only peak hours (08:00–20:00 CET Mon-Fri, excluding holidays). The decomposition must use the correct holiday calendar for the bidding zone to determine which hours are peak. Custom blocks (e.g., "morning" = 06:00–10:00) must be configurable per exchange. Block orders that are partially executed must be decomposed proportionally — 50% execution on a 20 MW block = 10 MW across all constituent intervals. |
| DA-VOL-03 | Nomination/scheduling volume | Volume nominated to TSO (via balancing group schedule) for physical delivery. Should match traded volume. Any deviation between nominated and traded volume constitutes a pre-delivery imbalance and must be flagged as a warning. The nomination volume is submitted to the TSO by the balancing responsible party (BRP) by the gate closure time (typically 14:30 CET D-1 for DA schedules in Germany). The system must track the nomination timestamp and the nominated volume per interval separately from the traded volume to enable the imbalance calculation in DA-SET-04. If the tenant operates multiple balancing groups, the nomination must be attributed to the correct BG based on the asset-to-BG mapping. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| DA-PRC-01 | Auction clearing price | Market clearing price per hourly/15-min contract as published by EPEX post-auction. This IS the deal price — no further price determination needed. The system must ingest the official EPEX price publication (available ~12:42 CET on D-1) and match each price to the corresponding traded contract. Prices are in €/MWh with 2 decimal places. The system must store both the traded volume and the clearing price as immutable auction results — these are regulatory records under REMIT and must not be editable after import. Price validation: EPEX DA prices historically range from -500 to +4,000 €/MWh. Prices outside this range should trigger a data quality alert but not be rejected (extreme prices have occurred, e.g., during energy crises). |
| DA-PRC-02 | Negative price handling | DA prices can go negative (frequent in high-wind/solar hours). System must handle negative €/MWh in all calculations without sign errors. A BUY at negative price means the buyer receives money for taking delivery — the cashflow direction reverses. All settlement, P&L, and position valuation calculations must correctly handle the sign: `price × volume` where price < 0 and volume > 0 yields a negative settlement amount, meaning the buyer is credited, not debited. The system must NOT auto-correct or flag negative prices as errors. Negative prices are increasingly common in the EU power market (Germany had ~300 hours of negative DA prices in 2023). Specific UI treatment: negative prices should be displayed in a distinct color and negative cashflows should be clearly labeled as credits/receipts. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| DA-SET-01 | Gross settlement | `price[t] × volume[t] × interval_hours` per interval. Sum across all intervals for daily total. For 15-min intervals: interval_hours = 0.25. For hourly: interval_hours = 1.0. The settlement amount is always in EUR (EPEX clears in EUR). Each interval produces one settlement line item. The daily settlement total = sum of all interval line items. For BUY trades: positive settlement = payment to exchange. For SELL trades: positive settlement = receipt from exchange. The system must generate settlement records per delivery day, not per trade — multiple trades for the same interval are netted at the exchange level. Rounding: settlement amounts rounded to 2 decimal places (EUR cents) per the ECC clearing rules. |
| DA-SET-02 | Payment due date | D+2 (EPEX ECC clearing). The exchange handles all netting and margin between members — the platform tracks settlement cashflows for internal accounting and cashflow forecasting purposes. Payment date = delivery date + 2 business days (using the TARGET2 banking calendar, not the market holiday calendar). If D+2 falls on a TARGET2 holiday, payment rolls to the next TARGET2 business day. The system must generate a cashflow record with: amount, currency (EUR), payment direction (pay/receive), payment date, counterparty (ECC), and reference to the source trades. This cashflow feeds into the treasury/liquidity forecasting module (PORT-10). |
| DA-SET-03 | Exchange fees | EPEX trading fee + ECC clearing fee per MWh. Configured per exchange, applied to gross settlement. Current EPEX DA fees: ~€0.04–0.07/MWh trading fee + ~€0.01–0.03/MWh clearing fee (varies by member tier and volume discounts). The system must support configurable fee schedules per exchange with effective dates (fees change periodically). Fees are applied to the absolute volume (not netted) — a BUY of 10 MWh and a SELL of 5 MWh in the same interval incurs fees on 15 MWh, not 5 MWh. Fees are a separate settlement line item, not netted into the energy settlement price. The fee amount must be tracked as a cost component in P&L reporting (it reduces the net P&L of the DA trading activity). |
| DA-SET-04 | Imbalance settlement | `(Nominated[t] − Actual_delivered[t]) × imbalance_price[t]`. Imbalance price published by TSO post-delivery. Settled monthly by TSO to BRP. Can be positive or negative. The imbalance price is the "Ausgleichsenergiepreis" (reBAP) in Germany, published by the 4 German TSOs via the ENTSO-E transparency platform, typically available D+1. The imbalance volume per 15-min interval = difference between the BRP's scheduled nomination and the metered delivery. Positive imbalance (over-delivery) means the BRP delivered more than nominated — compensated at the imbalance price (which can be above or below DA price). Negative imbalance (under-delivery) — the BRP must pay the imbalance price for the shortfall. The monthly TSO settlement invoice aggregates all 15-min imbalance cashflows for the month. The system must ingest TSO imbalance data, match it to the BRP's balancing group, and generate the imbalance settlement as a separate cashflow stream from the energy settlement. Imbalance costs are attributed to the portfolio/book responsible for the scheduling decision. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| DA-VAL-01 | Realized P&L | For DA, everything is realized at delivery. `(DA_price - portfolio_average_cost) × volume` or simply the gross cashflow if DA is the reference price. In most configurations, DA is used as the reference price (cost basis) for the portfolio — the DA trade itself has zero P&L against the DA reference. The P&L becomes meaningful when DA trades are compared against the portfolio's hedge prices or the PPA contract price. For example: if a PPA delivers 50 MWh at €55/MWh and the trader sells 50 MWh in DA at €68/MWh, the realized P&L on the combined position = `(68 - 55) × 50 = €650`. The system must support configurable reference price for P&L calculation: DA price, portfolio weighted average cost, or deal-specific contract price. Realized P&L is final and immutable once the settlement period closes (no mark-to-market adjustments). |
| DA-VAL-02 | Intraday P&L vs DA price | If the position was first established in DA and later adjusted in ID: `(ID_price - DA_price) × adjustment_volume`. This captures the cost or benefit of intraday position adjustments. For example: trader buys 30 MW in DA at €65/MWh, then sells 10 MW back in ID at €72/MWh. The intraday adjustment P&L = `(72 - 65) × 10 × 0.25h = €17.50` per quarter-hour interval. This P&L must be attributed separately from the original DA P&L to enable performance analysis of the intraday trading desk vs. the day-ahead desk. The system must link the ID adjustment trade to the original DA position to compute this differential correctly. |

> **Spec agent context:** DA is the simplest instrument. No MTM needed (settles immediately). No shaping needed (native granularity). No optionality. The primary complexity is imbalance settlement (DA-SET-04) which requires TSO data ingestion, balancing group attribution, and a separate monthly settlement cycle. Block order decomposition (DA-VOL-02) requires the holiday calendar and configurable block definitions per exchange. The exchange fee structure (DA-SET-03) must be configurable with historical effective dates. The DA price serves as the benchmark reference for most other instruments' P&L calculations.

## 2. Intraday (ID) — Exchange Continuous + Auction

**Exchange:** EPEX Spot intraday continuous (opens 15:00 D-1, closes 5 min before delivery) + XBID cross-border.  
**Granularity:** 15-min (DE, AT, NL, BE, FR), hourly (all).  
**Settlement:** Via ECC clearing.  
**Delivery:** Physical, same day or next day.  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 20–100 per day, often clustered around forecast updates and gate closures.  
**Position lifecycle:** Multiple executions per interval → aggregate → nominate → deliver → settle. Near-real-time position management.

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ID-VOL-01 | Trade-by-trade volume | Each ID trade is a separate execution with its own price and volume. Multiple trades can cover the same delivery interval — a trader might buy 10 MW at 14:00 and then sell 5 MW at 14:15 for the same delivery quarter-hour. Volume = sum of all trades per interval, respecting buy/sell direction. The system must accept trade confirmations from the exchange in near-real-time (via FIX protocol, API, or file-based feed), each containing: trade ID, execution timestamp, delivery interval, volume (MW), price (€/MWh), direction (buy/sell), exchange order ID. Each execution must be stored as a separate deal record (not aggregated at import) to maintain the regulatory audit trail — REMIT requires retention of individual trade executions. The system should display both the individual trade list and the netted position per interval. |
| ID-VOL-02 | Net position per interval | ID trades net against DA position. Net = DA_volume + Σ(ID_buy_volumes) − Σ(ID_sell_volumes) per interval. This is the final nominated position that goes to the TSO. The netting must happen at the 15-min interval level (not hourly, even if some ID trades are hourly — hourly trades are split into four equal 15-min intervals for netting purposes). The system must provide a real-time view of the current net position per interval, updating within seconds of each new ID trade execution. This net position drives the scheduling/nomination decision — the BRP submits this to the TSO before gate closure. If the net position changes after nomination (e.g., a late ID trade), the system must flag the deviation between nominated and current net position as a potential imbalance exposure. Gate closure times vary by market: Germany = 5 min before delivery start for each QH; some markets have multiple gate closures (e.g., XBID cross-border gate closure is earlier than domestic). |
| ID-VOL-03 | XBID cross-border volume | SIDC-coupled trades involve cross-border capacity allocation. Volume limited by available interconnector capacity. Track original zone and counterpart zone. Each XBID trade must record: originating bidding zone, destination bidding zone, allocated cross-border capacity reference, and the SIDC matching algorithm that paired the trade. Cross-border trades may have different settlement timing than domestic trades. The system must validate that the sum of cross-border trades for a given direction (import/export) does not exceed the allocated transmission capacity for that border and delivery period. If the exchange reports a trade that violates this, it should be flagged as a data quality issue (the exchange shouldn't allow it, but the platform should validate). XBID trade data comes from the SIDC platform via EPEX's post-trade reporting, typically with a slight delay vs. domestic trades. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ID-PRC-01 | Trade execution price | Each trade has its own execution price (continuous matching). No single clearing price — VWAP or last price used for reporting. Execution prices in the ID continuous market can vary significantly within seconds, especially close to delivery when forecast updates trigger rapid trading. The system must store the exact execution price per trade (not an average) for regulatory and audit purposes. Price range validation: same as DA (−500 to +4,000 €/MWh) but with additional alert thresholds for trades executed more than 20% away from the current DA price (potential fat-finger or erroneous trade). |
| ID-PRC-02 | VWAP calculation | Volume-Weighted Average Price per delivery interval across all ID trades: `Σ(price[i] × volume[i]) / Σ(volume[i])`. Used for index settlement and reporting. The VWAP must be calculated separately for buy and sell trades (buy VWAP and sell VWAP), as well as an all-trades VWAP. For indexed OTC or swap contracts that reference the "EPEX ID VWAP", the system must use the official EPEX-published VWAP (which may differ slightly from the tenant's own trades' VWAP). The system should compute both the tenant's own VWAP (from their trades) and display the market VWAP (from the exchange publication) side by side for comparison. The VWAP calculation must exclude cancelled or busted trades (trades voided by the exchange after execution). |
| ID-PRC-03 | ID auction price | Germany-specific 15:00 ID auction. Single clearing price per 15-min contract, similar to DA mechanism. This auction runs once daily for next-day delivery intervals and produces 96 clearing prices (one per QH). The system must ingest these prices separately from the continuous ID trades. ID auction trades are booked as separate deals from continuous trades — different price formation mechanism, different regulatory reporting. The auction clearing price may differ significantly from continuous trading prices for the same interval. The system should display both on the position view. |
| ID-PRC-04 | Negative price handling | Same as DA — ID prices frequently go negative, especially close to delivery in high-renewables hours. ID negative prices tend to be more extreme than DA because of the shorter time horizon and thinner liquidity. All sign-handling requirements from DA-PRC-02 apply equally. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ID-SET-01 | Per-trade settlement | `price × volume × interval_hours` per trade. Aggregated for clearing. Each trade produces a settlement amount. ECC aggregates all ID trades for a given delivery day into a single clearing settlement. The system must track both the per-trade settlement (for internal attribution and reporting) and the aggregate settlement (matching the ECC clearing statement). Reconciliation: the sum of all per-trade settlements must equal the ECC clearing amount for the period. Any discrepancy must be flagged and investigated — it typically indicates a missed trade import or a busted/cancelled trade not reflected. |
| ID-SET-02 | Payment due date | D+1 or D+2 depending on exchange/clearing rules. Track per trade. Current EPEX/ECC rules: ID trades for delivery day D are settled on D+1. This is one day faster than DA settlement. The system must correctly differentiate the payment timeline: a trader who buys in DA (settles D+2) and sells in ID (settles D+1) for the same delivery day has a one-day cashflow mismatch — the ID receipt arrives one day before the DA payment is due. This matters for liquidity forecasting. |
| ID-SET-03 | Imbalance settlement | Same as DA-SET-04. The final nominated position (DA + ID net) vs. actual metered delivery drives imbalance. The ID trades are the trader's last opportunity to adjust the position before delivery — effective ID trading minimizes imbalance costs. The system should provide analytics comparing: (a) imbalance cost without ID trading (DA position vs. actual), (b) imbalance cost with ID trading (final position vs. actual), and (c) ID trading cost/benefit (the P&L from the ID trades themselves). This demonstrates the value of intraday trading activity. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| ID-VAL-01 | Realized P&L | Like DA — fully realized at delivery. Per-trade P&L: `(execution_price - reference_price) × volume`. Reference = DA price or portfolio cost basis. The system must support multiple reference price options configurable per portfolio: (a) DA clearing price for the same interval — shows the cost/benefit of trading intraday vs. accepting the DA price, (b) portfolio weighted average cost — shows the P&L contribution of the ID trade to the overall portfolio, (c) PPA contract price — shows the margin capture on the combined origination + trading position. Each reference price option produces a different P&L number for the same trade — all are valid, used for different reporting purposes. |
| ID-VAL-02 | Pre-delivery unrealized P&L | For ID trades executed for future delivery intervals (e.g., next-day contracts traded at 15:00): unrealized until delivery. `(current_best_bid/ask - execution_price) × volume`. Short-lived — becomes realized within hours. The system should show this as "intraday unrealized" with a clear distinction from forward unrealized P&L (which spans months/years). The bid/ask reference for the unrealized calculation should use the latest available market data: the ID continuous order book best bid/ask if available, otherwise the last traded price for that interval. If the market is closed or no recent trades exist, fall back to the DA clearing price. This unrealized P&L is primarily useful for intraday risk monitoring — it tells the trader whether their open ID positions are currently profitable before delivery. |

> **Spec agent context:** ID complexity comes from multiple trades per interval (VWAP needed), near-real-time execution, and the netting against DA positions. No forward MTM needed (too short-lived). XBID cross-border trades add zone-pair tracking. The system must support near-real-time position updates (within seconds of trade execution) via exchange API/FIX feed integration. Gate closure time management is critical — the system must warn traders when gate closure is approaching and the position differs from nomination. The imbalance analysis (comparing with/without ID trading) is a key value-add for the platform.

## 3. OTC Bilateral (Fixed Price + Indexed)

**Counterparty:** Bilateral, governed by EFET General Agreement or ISDA master agreement.  
**Products:** Baseload, peak, off-peak, shaped.  
**Tenors:** Week, month, quarter, season, calendar year.  
**Settlement:** Physical delivery + financial settlement monthly in arrears per EFET terms.  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 20–100 active at any time, tenors from 1 week to 3 years.  
**Position lifecycle:** Negotiate → confirm → shape to position → deliver month by month → settle monthly → final reconciliation.

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OTC-VOL-01 | Contractual volume resolution | `contracted_MW × hours_in_period`. For baseload: all hours. For peak: Mon-Fri 08:00–20:00 CET excluding public holidays. For off-peak: all non-peak hours. Requires holiday calendar per market area. The hour classification must follow the standard EEX/EFET peak definition unless the contract specifies otherwise — some bilateral contracts use non-standard peak definitions (e.g., "extended peak" 07:00–23:00 or "super peak" 08:00–12:00, 17:00–20:00). The system must support custom peak/off-peak hour definitions per contract as an override to the market-area default. Volume calculation example: a Q1 2027 Peak Baseload at 25 MW in the DE-LU zone requires counting the exact peak hours in Q1, which depends on the DE public holiday calendar. Q1 2027 has 64 working days × 12 peak hours = 768 peak hours. Volume = 25 MW × 768h = 19,200 MWh. If the system uses an incorrect holiday calendar (e.g., missing "Tag der Deutschen Einheit"), the peak hour count and therefore the volume and settlement will be wrong. |
| OTC-VOL-02 | Shape decomposition | Annual/quarterly/monthly contracts must be shaped to daily and hourly/15-min for position aggregation with spot trades. Apply shape profiles (seasonal, day-type, intraday). The shaping must use the three-stage decomposition engine: (1) annual → monthly using seasonal shape factors, (2) monthly → daily using day-type profiles (working/Saturday/Sunday-holiday) + holiday calendar, (3) daily → hourly/15-min using intraday load/generation profile. Volume conservation must be validated at each stage: the sum of shaped sub-period MWh must equal the parent period MWh to 6 decimal places. If conservation fails, the shaping engine must log a warning and apply a correction factor to the last sub-period to force conservation. The shape profile used for each decomposition stage must be recorded with the volume series (for audit and recomputation), and the system must support re-shaping when a profile is updated (e.g., new seasonal factors based on updated weather outlook). |
| OTC-VOL-03 | Tolerance band | Some bilateral contracts include ±X% annual volume tolerance. If actual take is outside the band, penalty pricing applies to the excess/shortfall. The tolerance is typically defined as a percentage of the annual contracted volume. For example: 100,000 MWh/year with ±10% tolerance means the buyer must take between 90,000 and 110,000 MWh. If actual take = 85,000 MWh, the shortfall of 5,000 MWh (below the 90,000 floor) is settled at the contract price as a "deemed delivery" — buyer pays for energy not taken. If actual take = 115,000 MWh, the excess 5,000 MWh (above 110,000 cap) is settled at spot price instead of contract price. The system must track cumulative annual volume on a rolling basis, project the year-end position based on current delivery rate, and alert when the projection approaches a tolerance boundary. The tolerance calculation is annual — it resets each calendar year (or contract year if the deal anniversary is not Jan 1). |
| OTC-VOL-04 | Take-or-pay minimum | Minimum annual offtake commitment. Shortfall below minimum triggers payment at contract price for un-taken volume. Similar to tolerance band but without an upper cap — the buyer must take at least X MWh/year but can take unlimited excess. The take-or-pay obligation is typically a separate commercial term from the tolerance band (some contracts have both). The system must calculate the take-or-pay liability at each month-end: `max(0, minimum_mwh - cumulative_actual_mwh) × contract_price`. This liability is an accrual that reduces month by month as deliveries accumulate through the year. If the year ends with actual < minimum, the system must generate a take-or-pay settlement invoice for the shortfall. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OTC-PRC-01 | Fixed price | Agreed €/MWh for the delivery period. May differ by product type (base vs peak) within the same contract. A single OTC deal may have different prices for different delivery sub-periods: e.g., Cal 2027 at €62/MWh base and €78/MWh peak. The system must store price per product type per delivery period within the deal. If the contract has a single "all-hours" price, the system applies it to all intervals regardless of peak/off-peak classification. Fixed prices are immutable once the deal is confirmed — any price change requires a deal amendment (which creates a new version). |
| OTC-PRC-02 | Indexed price | Settlement against a published index (e.g., EPEX DA base average for the delivery month). Final price known only after delivery period. Formula: `index_value ± premium/discount`. The index reference must be precisely specified: which exchange, which product (base/peak/off-peak), which calculation method (arithmetic average of hourly prices, VWAP, settlement price), which bidding zone. For example: "EPEX Spot DE-LU day-ahead auction, monthly arithmetic average of all 24 hourly clearing prices" is a complete index specification. The premium/discount is in €/MWh, applied after the index is resolved. The system must ingest the official index values from the exchange publication (not calculate them from individual prices — the exchange's published average is the contractual reference). For months not yet delivered: the system uses the EEX forward curve as an estimate of the future index value for MTM purposes. This estimate is replaced by the actual index value after the delivery month completes and the exchange publishes the official average. |
| OTC-PRC-03 | Formula pricing | Complex price formulas: e.g., `0.7 × EEX_Base_M+1 + 0.3 × EPEX_DA_Avg + €2.50`. Must support configurable multi-component formulas with weights and offsets. The formula engine must support: (a) multiple index references in a single formula, (b) arithmetic operations (+, −, ×, ÷), (c) constant offsets and multipliers, (d) min/max functions (for floor/cap within the formula), (e) time-lagged references (e.g., "EEX settlement price from the 3rd business day before delivery month start"). Each index component in the formula resolves independently — some may be known (historical indices) while others are estimated (forward curves). The system must clearly distinguish between "firm" formula components (based on published indices) and "estimated" components (based on forward curves) in the MTM display. The formula definition must be stored as a structured expression tree (not free text) to enable programmatic evaluation. Formula validation: the system must verify that all referenced indices are available in the market data service before saving the deal. |
| OTC-PRC-04 | Price escalation | Annual escalation on the fixed component: `base_price × (1 + escalation_pct)^year_offset`. Compounds from deal effective date. Year_offset is calculated from the deal's start date, not the calendar year — a deal starting Jul 2026 escalates in Jul 2027, not Jan 2027. The escalation can be: (a) fixed percentage (e.g., 1.5%/year), (b) CPI-linked (e.g., EU HICP index, published by Eurostat with ~2 month lag — the system must handle the publication delay), (c) custom formula (e.g., 50% CPI + 50% PPI). For CPI-linked escalation, the system must specify which CPI index (EU HICP, German VPI, etc.), which base period, and the calculation methodology (year-on-year change or cumulative from base). The escalated price for each delivery year must be computed and stored in `deal_delivery_profile` at deal entry time and recomputed when CPI indices are updated. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OTC-SET-01 | Monthly invoice generation | Standard EFET: monthly settlement in arrears. Invoice = `Σ(price × volume × hours)` per delivery day within the month. Separate line items for base, peak, off-peak if applicable. The invoice must include: header (seller, buyer, contract reference, delivery month, invoice date, due date), line items (one per delivery day or one per product type per month depending on contract terms), and a total. For indexed OTC, the invoice cannot be generated until the monthly index is published — typically 3–5 business days after month end. The system must track invoice readiness per deal: "pending index" until the index is available, then auto-generate or queue for review. The invoice format must comply with EFET recommended formats and include all fields required by EU VAT regulations (reverse charge mechanism for cross-border energy transactions). The system should support both auto-generation (straight-through processing for standard deals) and manual review/approval workflows (for complex or high-value deals). |
| OTC-SET-02 | Payment due date | Per EFET General Agreement: typically 20th business day of the month following delivery (M+20BD). Configurable per contract. Business days are counted using the TARGET2 banking calendar, not the energy market calendar. Some contracts use calendar days instead of business days — the system must support both. The due date calculation must handle year-end and holiday periods correctly (December delivery invoiced in January, payment due in late January/early February — the TARGET2 calendar has closures around Christmas/New Year that can shift the due date). The system must generate cashflow forecasts based on calculated due dates across all OTC deals, enabling the treasury team to plan liquidity needs. Late payment triggers the interest calculation in OTC-SET-04. |
| OTC-SET-03 | Bilateral netting | Multiple deals with the same counterparty under the same master agreement: net receivables vs. payables. Single net payment per counterparty per settlement period. The netting must respect the netting agreement scope — not all deals with the same counterparty are necessarily under the same master agreement. The system must support netting groups: a netting group = one EFET or ISDA master agreement. All deals within a netting group are netted; deals in different netting groups with the same counterparty are settled separately. The netting calculation: `net_amount = Σ(receivables) − Σ(payables)` across all deals in the netting group for the settlement period. If net > 0: counterparty pays tenant. If net < 0: tenant pays counterparty. The netting statement must list all individual deal settlements that contribute to the net amount. Netting reduces counterparty credit exposure — the credit exposure is the net amount, not the gross receivable (this is important for OTC-VAL-07). |
| OTC-SET-04 | Late payment interest | EFET standard: EURIBOR + agreed margin (typically 200 bps) on overdue amounts, calculated daily. `overdue_amount × (EURIBOR + margin) / 365 × days_overdue`. The EURIBOR reference rate (typically 3-month EURIBOR) must be sourced from the ECB daily publication. The margin (in bps) is a contract term stored in the master agreement configuration. Interest accrues from the day after the payment due date until the actual payment date. The system must track payment receipts (matched against invoices) to determine overdue days. Interest is typically invoiced separately (monthly or quarterly) as a "late payment interest note". The system should auto-calculate accrued interest and either auto-generate interest invoices at configurable intervals or provide a report for manual invoicing. Some contracts specify a grace period (e.g., 5 business days) before interest starts accruing — this must be configurable. |
| OTC-SET-05 | Tax / levy pass-through | Some contracts pass through electricity tax (Stromsteuer DE: €20.50/MWh) and/or network charges. Separate invoice line items. Not all contracts include tax pass-through — it depends on the commercial structure and the parties' tax status (e.g., energy-intensive industrial buyers may have Stromsteuer exemptions). The system must support configurable tax/levy items per contract with: applicable tax type, rate (€/MWh or % of energy value), exemption flag, effective date range. Tax amounts are calculated on the delivered volume: `tax_amount = volume_MWh × tax_rate`. Tax line items appear on the settlement invoice separately from the energy settlement. The system must not apply taxes automatically based on the market area — it must use the contract-level tax configuration, because tax applicability varies by contract and counterparty. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OTC-VAL-01 | Forward MTM | `(forward_curve_price[t] − deal_price[t]) × volume[t]` summed across all undelivered intervals. Uses the latest EEX forward curve shaped to matching granularity. The forward curve must be shaped using the same decomposition engine and shape profiles as the deal volume (see OTC-VOL-02) to ensure granularity alignment. For peak products, the forward curve price must be the peak forward price (not base) — using the wrong product type in the curve produces a materially incorrect MTM. The MTM calculation must handle partial months: if today is March 15 and the deal delivers for all of March, the MTM covers only March 16–31 (the undelivered portion). Delivered intervals (March 1–15) move to realized P&L. The forward curve used for MTM must be timestamped and versioned — the system must record which curve version was used for each MTM snapshot to enable retrospective P&L attribution (which part of the P&L change was due to curve movement vs. time passage). |
| OTC-VAL-02 | Realized P&L | For delivered intervals: `(settlement_price − deal_price) × actual_volume`. For fixed-price OTC: settlement_price = deal_price, so realized P&L = 0 on the deal itself (value was captured at booking via MTM). This is a subtle but important point: for a fixed-price OTC deal, the realized P&L of the energy settlement is zero because the price received = price paid by definition. The value of the deal was captured at trade execution as "day-one P&L" — the difference between the market price at booking and the deal price. This day-one P&L sits in unrealized MTM from booking until delivery, then transitions to realized. The system must track this transition correctly: realized P&L grows daily as delivery intervals pass, unrealized P&L shrinks correspondingly, and the total (realized + unrealized) changes only with market price movements. For indexed-price OTC: realized P&L depends on the actual index value and is known only after the settlement index is published. |
| OTC-VAL-03 | Unrealized P&L | Current MTM of undelivered volume. Changes daily with forward curve movements. This is the primary daily P&L metric that traders and risk managers monitor. The unrealized P&L must be computed at end-of-day (using the EOD forward curve) and available in the dashboard by 18:00 CET. Intraday: the system should also provide an indicative unrealized P&L using the latest available curve (which may be mid-day), clearly labeled as "indicative" vs. the official EOD valuation. The unrealized P&L drives several downstream processes: (a) counterparty credit exposure monitoring (OTC-VAL-07), (b) portfolio risk reporting, (c) management P&L reporting. Changes in unrealized P&L from one day to the next feed into P&L attribution (OTC-VAL-04). |
| OTC-VAL-04 | P&L attribution | Decompose daily P&L change into: price effect (curve moved), volume effect (if applicable), new deal effect (day-one P&L), time decay (for long-dated). The attribution must be exhaustive: the sum of all attributed components must equal the total P&L change to within a rounding tolerance of €1. Attribution methodology: (1) price effect = `volume × (new_curve_price − old_curve_price)` — isolates the impact of forward curve movement, (2) volume effect = `(new_volume − old_volume) × old_curve_price` — isolates the impact of volume changes (e.g., forecast revision affecting shaped volumes), (3) time/roll effect = the P&L from one day's worth of delivery intervals moving from "undelivered" to "delivered" (from unrealized to realized), (4) new deal effect = day-one MTM of deals booked today, (5) amendment effect = MTM change from deal amendments. The system should produce a daily P&L attribution report per portfolio showing each component. Residuals (unexplained P&L) should be flagged — typically caused by non-linear effects or curve interpolation differences. |
| OTC-VAL-05 | NPV discount (long-dated only) | For OTC contracts > 2 years: discount future cashflows using EUR OIS/ESTR curve. `MTM_discounted = Σ(cashflow[t] × discount_factor[t])`. Immaterial for contracts < 1 year. The discount curve should be the EUR ESTR OIS swap curve (replacing the deprecated EONIA curve), sourced from a market data provider (e.g., Bloomberg, Refinitiv) or the ECB's published risk-free rate term structure. Discount factors are calculated as: `df(t) = exp(-r(t) × t)` where r(t) is the zero rate at time t (in years). For a 3-year contract, the discount impact is typically 2–5% of the undiscounted MTM. The system must support both discounted and undiscounted MTM views — some tenants may not want to apply discounting for internal reporting (it adds complexity) while regulators and auditors may require it. The choice should be configurable per portfolio. |
| OTC-VAL-06 | Credit valuation adjustment | Counterparty credit risk on positive MTM exposure. `CVA = PD × EAD × LGD`. Requires counterparty credit rating or internal scoring. PD (probability of default) is derived from the counterparty's credit rating: for an investment-grade (BBB) energy company, 1-year PD ≈ 0.2–0.5%. EAD (exposure at default) = positive MTM after netting. LGD (loss given default) typically assumed at 40–60% for unsecured energy OTC. The CVA is an adjustment to the deal's MTM — it reduces the value of positive exposures (you might not collect) and is reported separately in the P&L. CVA calculation frequency: at minimum monthly, preferably daily for large exposures. The system should alert when CVA exceeds a configurable threshold (e.g., > €50K or > 5% of gross MTM). For Phase 1 MVP, CVA can be simplified to a flat haircut percentage per counterparty credit tier, avoiding the full PD/LGD modelling. |
| OTC-VAL-07 | Counterparty exposure | Net MTM per counterparty after netting. Compare against credit limit. Alert if exposure > threshold. The exposure calculation must use the netted MTM within each netting group (not gross deal-level MTM) — this is consistent with how credit exposure is legally defined under EFET/ISDA netting agreements. The system must maintain a credit limit per counterparty (or per netting group), set by the credit/risk team. The exposure check should run: (a) on every new deal booking (pre-trade check: "will this deal cause the credit limit to be breached?"), (b) on every EOD MTM update (post-trade monitoring). If exposure > 80% of limit: warning. If exposure > 100% of limit: hard alert to risk manager. If exposure > 110%: block new deal booking with the counterparty until limit is increased or exposure reduces. The system should provide a counterparty exposure dashboard showing: current exposure, credit limit, % utilization, exposure trend (last 30 days), largest deal contributor to exposure. |

> **Spec agent context:** OTC is the most diverse instrument class. The key complexity drivers are: formula pricing (OTC-PRC-03 — requires a configurable expression tree engine), bilateral netting across deals (OTC-SET-03 — must respect netting group scope), EFET payment terms with late interest (OTC-SET-02/04), and forward MTM requiring shaped forward curves aligned to deal granularity. Indexed OTC (OTC-PRC-02) adds the dependency on post-delivery index publication, which delays invoice generation. The tolerance band (OTC-VOL-03) and take-or-pay (OTC-VOL-04) are annual calculations that require year-to-date volume tracking with year-end settlement. Two sub-variants (OTC-Fixed and OTC-Indexed) share volume/settlement logic but differ in price determination and invoice timing.

## 4. Power Purchase Agreement (PPA)

**Structure:** Pay-as-Produced (primary), Fixed Volume, Floor/Cap (Collar).  
**Tenor:** 5–25 years.  
**Settlement:** Monthly in arrears against metered generation.  
**Counterparty:** Bilateral, bespoke contract (typically not under EFET — custom PPA agreement).  
**Linked to:** Specific generation asset (wind farm, solar park).  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 5–50 PPAs, each linked to a specific asset. Long-lived — a 10-year PPA booked today stays active for 10 years.  
**Position lifecycle:** Negotiate → sign → asset generates → meter → settle monthly → annual reconciliation → 7+ year regulatory retention.  
**Complexity driver:** Volume uncertainty (weather-dependent), cannibalisation (price-volume correlation), long tenor (discounting), regulatory requirements (GoOs, curtailment compensation).

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| PPA-VOL-01 | Pay-as-produced volume | Contractual volume = actual metered generation of the linked asset at ISP granularity (15-min). Not known until delivery occurs. Forward-looking volume = P50 generation forecast. This is the fundamental characteristic of a PaP PPA: the buyer agrees to purchase whatever the asset produces — there is no guaranteed volume. For position management, the system must use the P50 generation forecast as the best estimate of future volume, while clearly distinguishing it from confirmed/actual volume. The metered generation data arrives from the asset's meter data management system (MDM) or the distribution system operator (DSO) — typically at D+1 for preliminary readings and after annual reconciliation for final readings. The system must support multiple quality levels of metered data (PRELIMINARY → VALIDATED → FINAL) and correctly propagate quality changes to settlement calculations. The quality-driven retention model (see data architecture section 11) ensures that 15-min metered data stays in Aurora until FINAL, enabling corrections at any point during the settlement window. |
| PPA-VOL-02 | P10/P50/P90 forecast | Probabilistic generation forecast from weather model. P50 = median expected generation (50% probability that actual will exceed this). P10 = downside case (90% probability actual exceeds this — i.e., only a 10% chance generation is this low). P90 = upside case (10% probability actual exceeds this). These forecasts are provided by specialist weather/forecast providers (Meteologica, EWC, DTN, Meteogroup) via API or file feed, typically updated every 1–6 hours for the short-term (D+0 to D+10) and daily/weekly for the medium-term. The system must ingest these forecasts, store them as volume_series with confidence_level = P10/P50/P90, and use P50 as the default for position management and MTM. P10 and P90 are used for risk calculations (PPA-VAL-08 CFaR). The system must track forecast accuracy over time: compare each forecast vintage against the actual outcome to build a track record per asset per forecast provider. This enables forecast provider comparison and helps calibrate risk models. Forecast accuracy metrics: MAE (Mean Absolute Error), RMSE (Root Mean Square Error), bias (systematic over/under-prediction). |
| PPA-VOL-03 | Shape decomposition (forward) | For the undelivered portion beyond the forecast horizon (typically > D+10): annual expected GWh → monthly (seasonal wind/solar profile) → daily → 15-min. Asset-specific shape profiles, not generic market profiles. The decomposition must use the asset's historical generation profile (ideally 3–5 years of metered data) to derive seasonal factors. A wind farm in northern Germany has a very different monthly generation pattern than a solar park in southern Spain — using a generic "wind" profile would produce material shaping errors. If the asset is newly commissioned with < 1 year of history, the system should fall back to: (a) the manufacturer's projected generation profile (from the project's energy yield assessment), (b) a technology + region cluster average from the forecast provider, (c) a flat profile as last resort (with a warning). The shape profile must be versioned — when the profile is updated with new historical data (typically annually), the system must re-shape all forward contractual volumes using the new profile and attribute any resulting position change to "profile update" in P&L attribution. |
| PPA-VOL-04 | Degradation adjustment | Annual capacity degradation: `effective_capacity = nameplate × (1 − degradation_rate)^years_since_commissioning`. Wind: ~0.3%/year. Solar: ~0.5%/year. Applied multiplicatively to forecast. Degradation is a physical phenomenon — turbine blades erode, solar panel efficiency decreases with UV exposure and thermal cycling. The degradation rate is typically a contract term (the PPA agreement specifies the assumed degradation rate for settlement purposes). The actual degradation may differ from the contractual assumption — some contracts include a "performance test" clause allowing the degradation rate to be updated based on measured performance. The system must apply the contractual degradation rate year-over-year and adjust the expected generation accordingly. For a 50 MW wind farm with 0.3%/year degradation, the effective capacity in year 8 = 50 × (1 − 0.003)^8 = 48.81 MW — a reduction of 1.19 MW. This may seem small but over 8 years of delivery it affects ~34,000 MWh of expected volume. |
| PPA-VOL-05 | Curtailment volume | TSO-mandated curtailment (Einspeisemanagement in DE) reduces delivered volume below generation capacity. Compensation rules vary by contract. Curtailment occurs when the grid cannot absorb all generated renewable energy — the TSO instructs the asset to reduce output. In Germany, the EEG (Renewable Energy Sources Act) guarantees compensation for curtailed energy, but PPA contracts may have their own curtailment provisions. The system must: (a) detect curtailment events from metered data (generation drops to near-zero or to a TSO-instructed level during high-wind/high-solar periods), (b) calculate the proxy generation — what the asset would have generated without curtailment, using reference weather data and the asset's power curve (see PPA-PRC-05), (c) calculate the curtailed volume = proxy_generation − actual_generation for each curtailed interval, (d) apply the contract-specific compensation rule: at PPA price, at market price, at zero, or a custom formula. Curtailment is becoming increasingly frequent in Germany (several GWh per year for large wind farms) and is a material financial factor in PPA valuation. |
| PPA-VOL-06 | Tolerance band / annual true-up | Some PPAs guarantee minimum annual delivery (e.g., ≥ 85% of P50). If actual < minimum: seller compensates buyer. If actual > cap (e.g., 115% of P50): excess at spot price instead of PPA price. Threshold percentages are contract terms. The tolerance band creates embedded optionality in the PPA — the seller effectively has a put option (protection against extreme low-generation years) and the buyer has a call option (excess generation at spot). The system must: (a) compute the annual P50 baseline from deal_delivery_profile, (b) track cumulative actual delivery through the year (YTD MWh), (c) project the year-end total based on remaining months' P50 forecast, (d) display the projected year-end position relative to the tolerance band (below floor / within band / above cap), (e) at year-end, calculate the true-up settlement: shortfall compensation = `max(0, floor_mwh − actual_mwh) × ppa_price`, excess settlement = `max(0, actual_mwh − cap_mwh) × (spot_price − ppa_price)`. The tolerance band settlement is an annual cashflow separate from the monthly energy settlement. |
| PPA-VOL-07 | Availability deduction | Scheduled maintenance windows where the asset is unavailable. Not counted as curtailment. Contract may specify maximum allowed unavailability days/year. Planned maintenance (e.g., turbine overhaul, transformer replacement) is scheduled in advance and is the seller's responsibility. The PPA contract typically allows X days/year of planned unavailability without penalty (e.g., 15 days). Beyond the allowance, excess unavailability may trigger penalty payments or a volume credit to the buyer. The system must: (a) accept maintenance schedule data (date range, affected capacity, planned/unplanned flag), (b) deduct maintenance periods from the generation forecast (reduce expected volume during maintenance windows), (c) track YTD unavailability days against the contractual allowance, (d) calculate penalty if the allowance is exceeded. Unplanned outages (e.g., lightning strike, component failure) are handled differently from planned maintenance — some contracts treat unplanned outages as force majeure (no penalty), others impose penalties. The classification must be configurable per contract. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| PPA-PRC-01 | Fixed PPA price with escalation | `price_year_n = base_price × (1 + annual_escalation)^n`. Base price and escalation rate are contract terms. Escalation may be CPI-linked (requires CPI index lookup — e.g., EU HICP from Eurostat, published with ~2 month lag) or fixed percentage. The escalated price applies to the entire delivery year — it does not change within a year. The system must compute and store the escalated price for each delivery year in `deal_delivery_profile` at deal entry and update when CPI indices become available. For long-term PPAs (10+ years), the cumulative effect of escalation is significant: a €55/MWh base with 1.5%/year escalation reaches €63.67/MWh in year 10 and €73.86/MWh in year 20. The MTM calculation must use the correct year-specific escalated price for each future delivery period, not the base price. Escalation start date may be the deal execution date, the commercial operation date (COD) of the asset, or a fixed date — configurable per contract. |
| PPA-PRC-02 | Floor/cap (collar) price | Settlement price = `max(floor, min(cap, market_price))`. Buyer pays the bounded price, not the raw market price. Floor and cap may escalate annually (same mechanism as PPA-PRC-01). The collar structure limits both upside and downside risk. From the buyer's perspective: if market price < floor, buyer pays the floor (overpays vs. market — this is the "insurance premium" for downside protection). If market price > cap, buyer pays the cap (saves vs. market — this is the benefit). The market price reference for the collar is typically the EPEX DA clearing price per delivery interval (not a monthly average — the collar applies interval by interval). The system must: (a) apply the collar formula to each 15-min delivery interval using the actual DA clearing price, (b) calculate the settlement amount as `Σ(collared_price[t] × generation[t] × 0.25h)`, (c) track the embedded option value: `collar_MTM = Σ(max(0, floor − fwd_price) × volume) − Σ(max(0, fwd_price − cap) × volume)` for the forward view. PPAs with collar structures have embedded optionality that affects their MTM — the collar floor is effectively a sold put and the cap is a sold call. For accurate MTM, the system should ideally use an option pricing model (Black-76) for the collar value, not just the intrinsic value. However, for Phase 1, intrinsic value (comparing forward curve to floor/cap) is an acceptable simplification. |
| PPA-PRC-03 | Capture price | Generation-weighted average market price: `Σ(market_price[t] × generation[t]) / Σ(generation[t])`. NOT the arithmetic average of market prices. Always ≤ base average for wind/solar due to merit order effect. Capture rate = capture price / base average price. This is the single most important PPA-specific metric. Wind generates more when it's windy, which is often when all wind farms generate more, which suppresses the market price (merit order effect / cannibalisation). As a result, the average price at which wind generation occurs is lower than the flat average market price. Historical capture rates for German onshore wind: ~80–90% of base average price. Solar: ~85–100% (better capture because solar peak overlaps with demand peak, but declining as solar penetration increases). For future periods, the capture price must be estimated. The system must: (a) for past periods: calculate actual capture price from metered generation × actual market prices (15-min granularity), (b) for future periods: estimate forward capture price from P50 generation forecast × shaped forward curve, with a capture rate adjustment factor (PPA-VAL-07). The capture rate adjustment is critical — using the base forward price without capture rate adjustment overstates the PPA value by 10–20% for wind assets. |
| PPA-PRC-04 | Negative price clauses | Many PPAs specify: if market price < 0 for N consecutive hours (typically 6h), buyer payment = €0 for those hours (not negative). Some PPAs: buyer pays the negative price. Contract-specific. This clause exists because German EEG subsidy payments are suspended during consecutive negative price periods (≥ 6 hours of negative prices under §51 EEG 2023). PPA prices often mirror this structure. The system must: (a) detect consecutive negative price periods in the DA market data (count consecutive 15-min intervals where DA price < 0 and convert to hours), (b) if the consecutive duration ≥ the threshold (contract term, typically 6h): set the PPA settlement price to €0 for all intervals in the negative period, (c) if < threshold: apply the normal PPA price (even though the market price is negative). The "consecutive" counting must respect the contract's definition: some contracts count from the first negative interval, others require all intervals in the period to be negative (a single positive interval resets the counter). The system must support both counting methods as a contract-level configuration. Negative price periods affect both settlement (PPA-SET-01) and forward MTM (PPA-VAL-01 — the expected frequency of negative price periods must be estimated for valuation). |
| PPA-PRC-05 | Proxy generation (for compensation) | If asset is curtailed or in outage: "what would have generated?" based on reference wind speed / irradiance data and the asset's power curve. Used for curtailment compensation calculation. The proxy generation model takes: (a) reference meteorological data (wind speed at hub height, wind direction, temperature for wind; global horizontal irradiance, plane-of-array irradiance, temperature for solar) from an independent met station or satellite-based service, (b) the asset's certified power curve (MW output as a function of wind speed, provided by the turbine manufacturer) or PV performance model, and (c) any applicable deductions (wake effects, electrical losses, transformer losses). The proxy calculation = `power_curve(reference_wind_speed) × (1 − losses) × turbine_count × availability_factor` per 15-min interval. The system must support configurable proxy methodologies per contract, as different PPA contracts may specify different reference data sources or calculation methods. The proxy generation is a disputed figure in practice — the system should store both the proxy calculation inputs and results to enable audit and dispute resolution. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| PPA-SET-01 | Monthly energy settlement | `Σ(ppa_price[t] × metered_generation[t] × 0.25h)` across all 15-min intervals in the month. This is the core invoice amount. The system must: (a) aggregate metered generation at 15-min resolution for the delivery month, (b) apply the correct PPA price for the year (including escalation from PPA-PRC-01), (c) apply negative price clause adjustments (PPA-PRC-04) — intervals where the clause triggers are settled at €0, (d) for collar PPAs: apply floor/cap to each interval's market price (PPA-PRC-02), (e) produce a settlement detail showing volume and value per day within the month, (f) generate the invoice with correct tax treatment (reverse charge for cross-border, with/without Stromsteuer depending on contract). The monthly settlement is preliminary if based on PRELIMINARY metered data — the invoice should be clearly marked as "preliminary" and subject to correction after final annual reconciliation. The system must track the preliminary→final settlement lifecycle and generate correction invoices when final metered data differs from preliminary. |
| PPA-SET-02 | Curtailment compensation invoice | If contract includes curtailment compensation: `Σ(proxy_generation[t] − actual_generation[t]) × compensation_price × 0.25h` for curtailed intervals. Separate invoice line item. The compensation price depends on the contract: (a) at PPA price — seller receives the same revenue as if the asset had generated normally, (b) at market price — seller receives the DA price for the curtailed volume (approximates what the seller would have earned on the spot market), (c) at a custom formula — e.g., 90% of the PPA price (risk-sharing). The curtailment compensation invoice must include: (a) identification of curtailed intervals (start/end timestamps, TSO dispatch instruction reference if available), (b) proxy generation calculation per interval, (c) actual generation per interval, (d) curtailed volume per interval (proxy − actual), (e) compensation price per interval, (f) total compensation amount. This is a contested area — sellers and buyers often disagree on proxy generation calculations. The system should provide full transparency on the proxy calculation methodology and inputs to support dispute resolution. |
| PPA-SET-03 | Tolerance band settlement | Annual calculation: if cumulative delivered MWh < minimum threshold: `(minimum_mwh − actual_mwh) × ppa_price` payable by seller. If > maximum: excess settled at spot. This settlement occurs once per year (or per contract anniversary), after the final annual generation data is available (typically M+14 after the settlement year end, aligned with the quality-driven retention model). The system must: (a) compute the annual delivered MWh from FINAL metered data, (b) compare against the tolerance band thresholds (PPA-VOL-06), (c) generate the true-up invoice for shortfall or excess, (d) if the tolerance band is based on P50 and the P50 estimate changes (e.g., due to profile update), track which P50 vintage is the contractual reference. The tolerance settlement is one of the last cashflows for a given delivery year — it arrives 14+ months after the year ends. The system must keep the delivery year's position "open" until the tolerance settlement is computed and booked. |
| PPA-SET-04 | GoO / certificate transfer | Guarantees of Origin (GoOs) bundled with energy delivery. Track GoO transfer per MWh delivered. If GoOs sold separately (unbundled PPA), separate financial settlement. GoO price per certificate as contract term or market price. Under EU Directive 2018/2001, GoOs certify that 1 MWh of energy was produced from a renewable source. In a bundled PPA, GoOs transfer with the energy — no separate pricing (the GoO value is embedded in the PPA price). In an unbundled PPA, energy and GoOs are settled separately — the system must generate two settlement streams. GoO transfer must be tracked in a certificate registry (e.g., AIB EECS, UBA Herkunftsnachweisregister in Germany). The system must record: (a) GoO certificate IDs or batch references, (b) volume (MWh) per transfer, (c) transfer date, (d) issuing body and technology type. For unbundled PPAs where GoOs are sold to a different party than the energy buyer: the GoO price may be a contract term (fixed €/GoO) or a market price reference (e.g., STX GoO index). The system must support GoO settlement as a separate cashflow with its own invoice and payment terms. |
| PPA-SET-05 | Payment due date | Monthly in arrears per contract terms. Typically M+15 to M+30 calendar days. Depends on meter data availability and invoice approval process. PPA payment terms are bespoke — unlike EFET-standardized OTC terms. The system must support fully configurable payment terms per contract: (a) calendar days or business days after month end, (b) from invoice date or from month end, (c) specific day of month (e.g., "25th of the month following delivery"), (d) grace period before late interest accrues. The payment timeline is often longer than OTC because PPA invoices require metered data verification, which can take 5–10 business days. The system should track the invoice lifecycle: generated → sent → acknowledged → approved → paid, with alerts for invoices stuck in a stage beyond configurable thresholds. |
| PPA-SET-06 | Preliminary vs final settlement | Preliminary settlement based on preliminary meter data (D+1). Final settlement after annual reconciliation (~M+14). Difference = settlement correction invoice. This is directly linked to the quality-driven retention model in the data architecture. The flow: (a) D+1: PRELIMINARY metered data arrives, quality_flag = PRELIMINARY. Monthly invoice generated from preliminary data. Buyer pays based on preliminary. (b) Monthly: meter data updated by DSO/TSO during monthly settlement process, quality_flag → VALIDATED. If the validated data differs materially from preliminary (e.g., meter correction, allocation factor change), a correction invoice is generated for the difference. (c) M+14: annual reconciliation completes, quality_flag → FINAL. Final correction invoice for any remaining differences. The system must track: the original preliminary settlement amount, each correction amount and its trigger (data quality change), and the cumulative final settlement amount. The sum of (preliminary + all corrections) must equal the final settlement. This correction chain is the primary reason 15-min metered data must stay in Aurora until FINAL — corrections apply at 15-min granularity and cannot be computed from daily aggregates. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| PPA-VAL-01 | PPA MTM (forward) | `Σ((forward_capture_price[t] − ppa_price[t]) × expected_volume[t])` across all undelivered intervals. **Critical:** must use forward capture price (not forward base price) because capture rate < 100%. The forward capture price = forward base price × estimated capture rate for the asset's technology and location. Using the base forward price without the capture rate adjustment will overstate the PPA value for wind by 10–20% and for solar by 5–15%. The capture rate itself is not constant — it varies by month (higher in summer for wind when wind generation share is lower vs. total demand) and by year (declining as renewable penetration increases). The system must: (a) compute forward capture price per delivery month using the shaped forward curve × month-specific capture rate factors, (b) multiply by expected volume (P50 forecast shaped to monthly), (c) apply the correct escalated PPA price per year, (d) discount the resulting cashflow stream for PPAs > 2 years (PPA-VAL-03). The MTM must be recalculated daily using the latest forward curve and P50 forecast. A significant forecast revision (e.g., new seasonal weather outlook) can move the PPA MTM materially — this must be attributed to "volume P&L" in the P&L decomposition (PPA-VAL-05). |
| PPA-VAL-02 | Cannibalisation / shape cost | The P&L drag from adverse price-volume correlation. `shape_cost = (base_average_price − capture_price) × expected_volume`. This is a material valuation component — can be 10-20% of gross value for wind. Cannibalisation is the core financial challenge of renewable PPAs: as more wind/solar capacity is installed, the price depression during high-generation hours deepens, reducing the capture rate over time. The system must: (a) compute realized shape cost for delivered periods: `(actual_base_avg − actual_capture) × actual_volume`, (b) estimate forward shape cost: `(forward_base_avg − forward_capture) × P50_volume`, (c) track shape cost trends over time — is the capture rate declining as expected or faster/slower than forecast? Shape cost is a separate line item in P&L reporting, distinct from the energy margin. Total PPA P&L = energy margin (base price × volume) − shape cost − hedge costs ± imbalance costs. A PPA with a 30% capture rate drag requires the PPA price to be 30% below the base forward to break even — this is why PPA pricing always involves a discount to the base forward curve. |
| PPA-VAL-03 | NPV discounting | Mandatory for PPAs > 2 years. `MTM_npv = Σ(cashflow[t] × discount_factor[t])` using EUR ESTR/OIS curve. For an 8-year PPA, discounting can reduce MTM by 5-15% vs. undiscounted. The discount curve must be the EUR ESTR OIS swap curve, sourced from a market data provider. For very long-dated PPAs (15–25 years), the discount curve may not have liquid swap rates beyond 10–15 years — the system must extrapolate the curve beyond the last liquid point using a configurable methodology (flat extrapolation, Nelson-Siegel, Smith-Wilson). The discount factor must be applied per delivery month: `df(month_m) = exp(-zero_rate(T_m) × T_m)` where T_m is the time in years from valuation date to the midpoint of delivery month m. The system must recompute discount factors daily when the ESTR curve updates. The impact of discount rate changes on MTM should be attributed to "rho P&L" or "discount P&L" in the P&L decomposition. |
| PPA-VAL-04 | Realized P&L | For delivered intervals: `(metered_volume × ppa_price) − (metered_volume × capture_price_equivalent)`. Or decomposed: energy margin + shape cost + curtailment compensation. The realized P&L for a PPA is the actual cash margin earned on delivered energy. Decomposition: (a) gross revenue = ppa_price × delivered_volume, (b) market value of delivered energy = Σ(DA_price[t] × delivered_volume[t]) — this is what the energy was worth on the spot market, (c) energy margin = gross revenue − market value (positive if PPA price > capture price, meaning the buyer overpaid vs. spot — this is the PPA's embedded value), (d) shape cost = base_avg_price × total_volume − Σ(DA_price[t] × delivered_volume[t]) — the cannibalisation impact (negative), (e) curtailment compensation = separately settled per PPA-SET-02. The sum of (c) + (d) + (e) should reconcile to the total realized P&L. The system must produce this decomposition monthly for management reporting and cumulatively for the deal's lifetime. |
| PPA-VAL-05 | Volume P&L | P&L change from forecast revision: `(new_forecast − old_forecast) × (forward_price − ppa_price)`. Material when weather outlook changes significantly. Volume P&L isolates the impact of changing generation expectations on the PPA's value. Example: a new seasonal weather outlook reduces expected wind generation by 5% for Q4. If the PPA price is below the forward price (PPA is valuable), lower volume reduces the PPA's value — negative volume P&L. If the PPA price is above the forward price (PPA is underwater), lower volume reduces the loss — positive volume P&L. The system must track forecast vintages and compute the delta between consecutive P50 updates to produce the volume P&L attribution. Volume P&L should be reported separately from price P&L — it answers the question "did the weather forecast change?" vs. "did the market price change?" |
| PPA-VAL-06 | Price P&L | P&L change from forward curve movement: `expected_volume × (new_forward_capture − old_forward_capture)`. Isolates the market price impact. Price P&L uses a constant volume assumption (yesterday's P50 forecast) and varies only the forward capture price. The capture price change incorporates both the base forward curve movement and any change in the estimated capture rate. The system must decompose price P&L further if the capture rate estimation methodology produces a time-varying capture rate: (a) base curve P&L = volume × (new_base − old_base) × capture_rate, (b) capture rate P&L = volume × new_base × (new_capture_rate − old_capture_rate). This second-order decomposition is useful for understanding whether the PPA's value change is driven by the overall market or by the cannibalisation dynamic specifically. |
| PPA-VAL-07 | Capture rate forecast | Forward-looking capture rate estimation. Historical capture rate × technology-specific trend factor (cannibalisation increasing as renewable penetration grows). Used in PPA-VAL-01. The capture rate forecast is one of the most critical and uncertain inputs to PPA valuation. Methods: (a) historical average — simple, but doesn't account for increasing cannibalisation, (b) historical trend extrapolation — assumes the historical decline rate continues, (c) fundamental modelling — uses a power system dispatch model to simulate future price profiles under different renewable penetration scenarios. For Phase 1, method (b) is recommended: compute the capture rate for the last 3 years, fit a linear trend, extrapolate forward. The system must allow the capture rate forecast to be overridden per deal (trader judgment) and must record the methodology and parameters used for audit. The capture rate forecast significantly affects PPA MTM — a 2% change in forward capture rate on a 50 MW 8-year PPA can move the MTM by €500K+. |
| PPA-VAL-08 | Cash Flow at Risk (CFaR) | Probabilistic distribution of future PPA cashflows. Inputs: P10/P50/P90 volume scenarios × forward price scenarios (Monte Carlo or historical simulation). Output: worst-case annual cash requirement at 95% confidence. CFaR answers the question: "what is the maximum cash we might have to outlay in a given year from this PPA portfolio?" This is critical for treasury planning because PPAs create exposure on both sides: (a) in high-wind, low-price years: the PPA delivers large volume at above-market prices — high cash outflow from the buyer, (b) in low-wind years: tolerance band shortfall payments from the seller. The system must run scenario combinations: P10 volume × low price, P50 volume × central price, P90 volume × high price, and tail combinations. Output: a probability distribution of annual cashflows with percentiles (P5 worst case, P25, P50 median, P75, P95 best case). CFaR is computed at portfolio level (PORT-05) but requires PPA-specific volume scenarios as inputs. |
| PPA-VAL-09 | Counterparty exposure | Net MTM exposure to PPA counterparty. Long-term PPAs create large exposure. Must track against credit limit and collateral/guarantees posted. PPA counterparty exposure is structurally different from OTC: (a) PPA tenors are much longer (10+ years vs. 1–3 years for OTC), creating larger peak exposure, (b) PPA counterparties are often project companies (SPVs) with limited credit standing — the credit support typically comes from a parent guarantee or a letter of credit, (c) the exposure can switch direction over the PPA's life: when market prices > PPA price, buyer has positive MTM (counterparty risk on the seller/SPV); when market prices < PPA price, the seller has the exposure risk. The system must track: (a) gross MTM exposure, (b) credit support (guarantees, LCs) and their expiry dates, (c) net exposure = gross − credit support, (d) compare against credit limit. Alert when net exposure > limit or when credit support is about to expire (e.g., LC renewal due within 60 days). |

> **Spec agent context:** PPA is the most complex instrument on this platform. Unique calculations not found in any other instrument: capture price (PPA-PRC-03), cannibalisation/shape cost (PPA-VAL-02), curtailment compensation with proxy generation (PPA-SET-02 + PPA-PRC-05), degradation adjustment (PPA-VOL-04), tolerance bands with annual true-up (PPA-VOL-06 + PPA-SET-03), negative price clause with consecutive-hour counting (PPA-PRC-04), GoO/certificate tracking (PPA-SET-04), preliminary/final settlement correction cycles (PPA-SET-06). The forward MTM MUST use capture price, not base price — this is the most common PPA valuation error in the industry. Pay-as-produced volume uncertainty (P10/P50/P90) drives CFaR. Settlement has preliminary/final correction cycles aligned with the quality-driven retention model. The capture rate forecast (PPA-VAL-07) is the single most sensitive assumption in PPA valuation — the system should make it transparent, auditable, and overridable.

## 5. Financial Swap

**Structure:** Fixed-for-floating exchange of cashflows. No physical delivery.  
**Fixed leg:** Agreed €/MWh. **Floating leg:** Published index (EPEX DA base, EEX settlement).  
**Clearing:** ECC (exchange-cleared) or bilateral (OTC).  
**Tenors:** Month, quarter, season, calendar year.  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 10–30 active swaps at any time.  
**Position lifecycle:** Execute → daily margin (if cleared) → delivery month → floating leg determined → final settlement.

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| SWP-VOL-01 | Notional volume | `contracted_MW × hours_in_period`. Baseload or peak, same hour classification as OTC. This is the notional, not physical delivery — no nomination needed. The notional volume must be calculated using the correct peak hour count for the delivery period, including the appropriate holiday calendar. For a Cal 2027 Baseload swap at 10 MW: notional = 10 × 8,760 = 87,600 MWh. For a Q2 2027 Peak swap at 10 MW: notional = 10 × (peak hours in Q2 2027 using DE holiday calendar) — the exact peak hour count depends on which days are holidays. The system must compute this identically to the future/OTC volume calculation (FUT-VOL-02, OTC-VOL-01). The notional volume determines the cashflow magnitude — getting it wrong by even one holiday (12 peak hours) on a €70/MWh swap at 10 MW = €8,400 settlement error. |
| SWP-VOL-02 | Shape decomposition | Same as OTC-VOL-02. Quarterly/annual notional shaped to monthly/daily for position aggregation and MTM. Swaps are often used as hedges against PPAs — for position management, the swap's shaped volume must be subtracted from the PPA's shaped volume at matching granularity to compute the portfolio's open (unhedged) position. The shape profiles used for the swap decomposition should ideally match the profiles used for the corresponding PPA hedge — using different profiles for the hedge and the hedged item creates artificial basis risk in the position view. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| SWP-PRC-01 | Fixed leg price | Agreed at trade execution. Fixed for the swap's lifetime. The fixed price typically reflects the market's expectation of the future floating index at the time of execution, adjusted for credit/liquidity premium. The system stores this as the deal price and uses it unchanged for the life of the swap. If the swap is amended (rare), a new fixed price may be agreed — this creates a deal amendment event, same as OTC-PRC-01. |
| SWP-PRC-02 | Floating leg price | Determined by the reference index over the delivery period. Typically: arithmetic average of EPEX DA hourly prices for the delivery month (for base) or peak hours only (for peak). Not known until delivery month completes. The floating leg index specification must be precise (same requirements as OTC-PRC-02): exchange, product, calculation method, bidding zone. For base swaps: the floating leg = arithmetic average of all 24 EPEX DA hourly clearing prices for each day in the delivery month, then averaged across all days. For peak swaps: same but only peak hours (as defined by the holiday calendar). The system must ingest the official EPEX monthly average publication and match it to each swap's floating leg specification. Some swaps reference the EEX monthly settlement price instead of EPEX DA — these are different numbers and the system must support both. The floating leg is a critical settlement input — using the wrong index or wrong averaging method will cause settlement disputes with the counterparty. |
| SWP-PRC-03 | Floating leg estimation (pre-delivery) | For undelivered periods: floating leg estimated from forward curve. `estimated_floating = forward_curve_price` for the delivery period at matching granularity. The forward curve provides the market's current expectation of what the floating leg will be. As the delivery period approaches and more DA prices are realized, the estimation becomes increasingly accurate. For a partially delivered month (e.g., today is March 15), the floating leg estimate is a blend: `(Σ(actual_DA[t] for t in 1..15) + Σ(forward_estimate[t] for t in 16..31)) / total_hours`. The system must compute this blended estimate daily for active delivery months and update the MTM accordingly. The transition from estimated to actual floating leg is the primary driver of swap P&L realization. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| SWP-SET-01 | Net settlement amount | `(fixed_price − floating_price) × notional_volume_MWh`. If positive: floating payer (buyer of swap) pays fixed payer. If negative: fixed payer pays. Single net cashflow per settlement period. The swap is a difference contract — only the net difference is exchanged, not the gross amounts. For a 10 MW Base swap at fixed €65/MWh, if the monthly EPEX DA average = €72/MWh, the floating payer pays: `(65 − 72) × 7,440 = −€52,080` — the fixed payer receives €52,080 because the market was above the fixed price. The system must correctly handle the sign: the "buyer" of a swap (who pays fixed, receives floating) benefits when the market rises. The settlement amount is computed after the delivery month completes and the floating index is published. The system should auto-compute the settlement as soon as the index is available and queue it for invoice generation. |
| SWP-SET-02 | Payment due date (bilateral) | Per ISDA/EFET terms: typically M+10 to M+20 BD after delivery month end. Floating leg must be determinable first (requires index publication). The payment timeline for bilateral swaps is: delivery month ends → index publication (3–5 BD) → settlement calculation → invoice generation → payment due (M+10 to M+20 BD). If the index publication is delayed (rare but possible), the settlement and payment timeline shifts accordingly. The system must track the index availability status and alert if the index for a completed delivery month is overdue. For exchange-cleared swaps, the payment timing is different — see SWP-SET-03 (daily variation margin). |
| SWP-SET-03 | Variation margin (cleared) | For ECC-cleared swaps: daily variation margin = `today_MTM − yesterday_MTM`. Cash settled daily via margin account. The variation margin is the daily change in the swap's mark-to-market value. The exchange (ECC) calculates this using its official settlement prices and calls/pays margin daily. The system must: (a) compute its own estimate of the daily MTM change (using the same settlement prices as ECC), (b) reconcile against the ECC margin statement (received daily), (c) flag any discrepancy > configurable threshold (typically > €100). The variation margin cashflow must be recorded separately from the final delivery settlement — VM is a series of daily cashflows throughout the swap's life, while the final settlement is a single cashflow after delivery. For accounting purposes, some firms treat VM as realized P&L (because cash has moved); others treat it as collateral. The system should support both treatments as a configurable accounting policy. |
| SWP-SET-04 | Initial margin (cleared) | Collateral posted to ECC. Calculated by exchange using SPAN/VaR. Not a P&L item but impacts cash/collateral management. Changes with position size and market volatility. The system must: (a) ingest the ECC's daily initial margin requirement per cleared swap, (b) track margin posted vs. required, (c) alert when additional margin is needed (margin call), (d) include IM requirements in the cashflow forecast (PORT-10) as contingent cash outflows. Initial margin is returned when the position is closed or the swap settles. For portfolio margining across multiple cleared instruments, ECC may net margin requirements — the system should track both the gross per-swap margin and the net portfolio margin. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| SWP-VAL-01 | Swap MTM | `Σ((forward_floating[t] − fixed_price) × volume[t] × discount_factor[t])` across all undelivered periods. For short-dated swaps (< 1 year): discounting immaterial, can omit. The MTM calculation is structurally similar to OTC-VAL-01 but the deal price is compared against the estimated floating leg (from the forward curve) rather than directly against the forward price. For base swaps, the forward floating estimate is the EEX base forward for the delivery month. For peak swaps, it's the EEX peak forward. The system must use the correct forward product — matching the swap's floating leg specification. The MTM should be computed at minimum daily (EOD) and optionally intraday using indicative forward curves. |
| SWP-VAL-02 | Realized P&L | For delivered months: `(fixed − actual_floating) × volume`. Known with certainty once the floating index for the month is published. Realized P&L for a swap is the clearest of all instruments — it's the simple difference between the agreed fixed price and the actual market outcome, times volume. The system must transition each delivery month from unrealized to realized as the floating index becomes available. The realized P&L per month should be displayed alongside the settlement amount — they should be identical (the settlement IS the realized P&L for a swap). |
| SWP-VAL-03 | Unrealized P&L | Current MTM of undelivered periods. = SWP-VAL-01. Unrealized P&L for swaps is sensitive to forward curve movements — a 1 €/MWh change in the forward curve for a 10 MW annual swap = ~€87,600 MTM change. The system must provide scenario analysis: "what happens to my swap MTM if the forward moves ±€5?" This is a basic sensitivity measure (delta × €5 shock) and should be available on the position dashboard. |
| SWP-VAL-04 | P&L attribution | Price P&L (curve movement) + time decay (for discounted swaps) + new deal P&L (day-one). Same methodology as OTC-VAL-04 but simpler because swaps have no volume optionality (fixed notional). The P&L attribution for a swap should reconcile to the total P&L change: `total_change = price_pnl + time_pnl + new_deal_pnl + rounding_residual`. The residual should be < €1 for a correctly implemented attribution. |

> **Spec agent context:** Swaps are financially settled — no physical delivery, no nomination, no imbalance. The primary complexity is: (1) floating leg requires the delivery-month index to be finalized before settlement (SWP-PRC-02 — the system must track index availability and auto-compute settlement when ready), (2) cleared vs. bilateral determines margin treatment (SWP-SET-03/04 — daily VM reconciliation against exchange is critical), (3) MTM requires discounting for long-dated swaps. Swaps are frequently used as hedging instruments against PPAs — cross-referencing swap P&L with PPA P&L is important for hedge effectiveness reporting (PORT-03). The system must track whether a swap is designated as a hedge of a specific PPA for IFRS 9 hedge accounting purposes.

## 6. Exchange Future

**Exchange:** EEX (Leipzig), ICE Endex, Nasdaq Commodities.  
**Products:** Baseload, peakload.  
**Tenors:** Week, month, quarter, season, calendar year (up to Cal+6).  
**Clearing:** ECC. **Settlement:** Financial (cash-settled) or physical (cascading into spot delivery).  
**Margining:** Daily variation margin + initial margin.  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 20–100 active futures positions at any time.  
**Position lifecycle:** Execute on exchange → daily margining → cascade at expiry → delivery or financial settlement.

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| FUT-VOL-01 | Contract volume (lots) | `lots × lot_size_MW × hours_in_delivery_period = MWh`. EEX German power: 1 lot = 1 MW. Hours depend on base vs. peak and the specific delivery period. The lot size may differ by exchange: EEX = 1 MW, ICE Endex = 1 MW, Nasdaq = varies. The system must support configurable lot sizes per exchange and product. The MWh calculation is identical to OTC-VOL-01 (same peak hour counting rules and holiday calendar requirements). For a Cal 2027 Base future: 1 lot × 1 MW × 8,760h = 8,760 MWh. For a Q2 2027 Peak future: 1 lot × 1 MW × (peak hours in Q2 per DE calendar). The system receives trade confirmations from the exchange (typically via FIX protocol or API) with: trade ID, execution timestamp, product code, lots, price, buy/sell. The product code must be mapped to the system's internal delivery period and product type definitions. |
| FUT-VOL-02 | Peak hour count | Peak = Mon-Fri 08:00–20:00 CET, excluding public holidays for the delivery zone. Must use the correct holiday calendar to count peak hours accurately. Wrong count = wrong volume = wrong P&L. This is identical to OTC-VOL-01 peak calculation and is listed separately because it's the single most common source of reconciliation breaks with the exchange. The EEX publishes the official peak hour count for each product — the system should cross-validate its calculated peak hours against the exchange-published count. If there's a discrepancy, the exchange's count is the contractual reference. Discrepancies typically arise from: (a) using the wrong year's holiday calendar, (b) missing a holiday (e.g., regional holiday that affects the national count), (c) using the wrong time zone for the hour boundary (CET, not local time). The system must use CET (Central European Time, not CEST) for all peak/off-peak hour boundary definitions, per EEX convention. |
| FUT-VOL-03 | Shape decomposition | Cal → quarters → months → daily → hourly/15-min for position aggregation. Same cascade as OTC. The decomposition is required to compare futures positions against PPA/OTC positions at matching granularity. A Cal 2027 Base future at 10 MW is a flat 10 MW for all 8,760 hours — when compared against a shaped PPA position, the system must be able to show the hourly/15-min net position (PPA generation profile minus flat future = open position that varies by hour). |
| FUT-VOL-04 | Cascade expiry roll | When a Cal future expires: the position conceptually "cascades" into the quarterly contracts. When a quarterly expires: into monthly. This is a position management operation, not a market event — the CTRM must handle the roll and resulting P&L attribution. The cascade happens automatically at EEX on the last trading day of the product: Cal 2027 expires on the last trading day of December 2026, and the holder's position "becomes" Q1+Q2+Q3+Q4 2027 quarterly positions. The financial settlement of the Cal vs. the sum of quarters should net to zero (the exchange ensures this through the cascade mechanism). However, the CTRM must: (a) close out the Cal position at the cascade settlement price, (b) open new Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 positions at their respective cascade opening prices, (c) attribute the P&L correctly: any difference between the Cal settlement value and the sum of quarterly opening values is "cascade basis risk" — it should be zero in theory but may have small rounding differences. (d) Maintain the link between the original Cal trade and the resulting quarterly positions for audit trail. The cascade is recursive: when Q1 2027 expires in December 2026, it cascades into Jan/Feb/Mar monthly futures. The system must handle multi-level cascades: Cal → Q → M automatically. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| FUT-PRC-01 | Trade execution price | Exchange execution price at the time of trade. Each execution is at a specific price (limit order filled at limit price, market order filled at best available). The system must store the exact execution price per trade for regulatory reporting (EMIR) and P&L calculation. |
| FUT-PRC-02 | Daily settlement price | Published by exchange (EEX) at end of each trading day (typically ~17:30 CET). Used for variation margin calculation and MTM. The EEX settlement price is determined by the exchange's settlement price methodology (typically based on the volume-weighted average of trades in the closing period, or a theoretical price if insufficient trading activity). The system must ingest these settlement prices daily and reconcile its MTM against the exchange's margin statement. The settlement price is the definitive reference for all futures valuation — the system should not use its own internal price estimates for official MTM; only the exchange-published settlement price. |
| FUT-PRC-03 | Final settlement price | For financially settled futures: average of the reference spot index over the delivery period (same as swap floating leg — see SWP-PRC-02). For physically settled: cascades to spot delivery obligations. Most EEX power futures are physically settled via cascade — they cascade into the DA auction rather than settling against an index average. The "final settlement" is then the DA auction prices over the delivery period. For financial settlement (some ICE Endex products): the final settlement price = average of the DA clearing prices for the delivery period, computed after the last delivery day. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| FUT-SET-01 | Variation margin (daily) | `(today_settlement_price − yesterday_settlement_price) × volume_MWh`. Positive = margin received. Negative = margin paid. Settled daily via ECC margin account. This IS the realized P&L for futures (mark-to-market settlement). The variation margin for futures is calculated and settled by ECC every business day. For the first day after trade: VM = (today_settlement − execution_price) × volume. For subsequent days: VM = (today_settlement − yesterday_settlement) × volume. The cumulative VM from trade date to today = current MTM of the position. The system must: (a) compute its own VM estimate using the exchange settlement prices, (b) reconcile daily against the ECC margin statement (received by 08:00 CET the following day), (c) flag discrepancies > €100. The VM cashflow must be recorded as a daily cashflow entry in the system — it's real cash moving in/out of the margin account. The aggregate VM across all futures positions is a significant daily cash requirement — can be millions of EUR for a portfolio of 50+ futures positions on volatile days. |
| FUT-SET-02 | Initial margin | Collateral requirement calculated by ECC using SPAN methodology. Not P&L — cash/collateral management. Changes with position size and market volatility. Same treatment as SWP-SET-04. The IM requirement increases with: (a) larger position size, (b) higher market volatility (ECC adjusts SPAN parameters during high-vol periods), (c) concentrated positions (less diversification benefit). The system must track IM requirements and alert when: (a) margin call is expected (current IM + buffer < required), (b) available collateral is insufficient, (c) IM requirements have increased significantly (e.g., > 20% increase in a day, indicating the exchange has tightened margin parameters). |
| FUT-SET-03 | Delivery settlement (physical) | If physically settled: at expiry, future cascades to spot delivery. Settlement against the spot reference price over the delivery period. `(execution_price − reference_spot_average) × volume`. The cascade into spot delivery means the futures holder receives/pays the difference between their futures price and the DA auction price for each delivery interval. The cascade mechanism ensures the futures holder's total economics = (futures price − DA average) × volume, same as a financial swap. The system must track the cascade delivery obligations and match them against DA trading activity. |
| FUT-SET-04 | Exchange fees | EEX trading fee + ECC clearing fee per lot. Configurable per exchange and product. Same requirements as DA-SET-03. Futures fees are typically per lot per trade (not per MWh) — the system must convert: `total_fee = lots × fee_per_lot`. Fee schedules vary by member tier and may have volume discounts. The system should track cumulative monthly trading volume per exchange to apply the correct fee tier. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| FUT-VAL-01 | Forward MTM | `(current_settlement_price − execution_price) × remaining_volume`. Because futures are daily-margined, the MTM already flows through variation margin. The "unrealized" portion shrinks daily as margin settles. In theory, the unrealized P&L of a fully-margined future is always zero (because all MTM changes are settled daily as VM). In practice, the system should show the "original MTM" = (current settlement − original execution) × volume to give the trader a sense of total profit/loss since inception, while also tracking that this amount has been received/paid through VM. The distinction matters for: (a) performance reporting (how profitable was this trade?), (b) hedge effectiveness (what is the cumulative hedge value?). |
| FUT-VAL-02 | Realized P&L | For futures: realized P&L = cumulative variation margin received/paid from trade date to valuation date. Unlike OTC where realized = delivered, futures realize through daily margining. The system must accumulate VM cashflows to produce the cumulative realized P&L. This is unique to exchange-cleared instruments — the entire P&L is "realized" in the cash flow sense, even though the delivery period may not have started yet. For reporting purposes, the system should distinguish between: (a) "margining-realized" P&L = cumulative VM (cash has moved), (b) "delivery-realized" P&L = applicable only after the delivery period, showing the actual energy settlement economics. Some tenants report using (a), others using (b) — the system should support both as a reporting preference. |
| FUT-VAL-03 | Roll P&L | When rolling a position (closing front-month, opening next-month): `(close_price − open_price) × volume`. Must attribute this correctly as roll cost, not market P&L. The roll is two separate trades (a close and a re-open) and the P&L decomposition must treat them as linked. The roll cost/benefit is a structural feature of the forward curve — contango (next-month > front-month) creates a roll cost for long positions, backwardation creates a roll benefit. The system must: (a) link the closing and opening trades as a "roll" trade pair (user action or automatic detection based on same-day close+open of adjacent delivery months), (b) compute roll P&L as a separate attribution category in the P&L report, (c) track cumulative roll costs per position over time (total cost of maintaining the hedge through repeated rolling). |
| FUT-VAL-04 | Cascade P&L attribution | At expiry cascade (Cal → Q, Q → M): P&L attribution between the expired product and the cascade products. The sum must reconcile. The cascade creates a "synthetic" P&L event: the Cal position is closed at the cascade settlement price, and new quarterly positions are opened at their cascade opening prices. The system must: (a) compute the P&L on the closed Cal position: (cascade_settlement − execution_price) × volume, (b) recognize that this P&L has already been paid out through cumulative VM — so the cascade event itself should produce zero additional cash, (c) the new quarterly positions open with a zero unrealized P&L (their execution price = the cascade opening price), (d) verify reconciliation: sum of quarterly opening values = Cal cascade settlement value. If there's a residual, it's cascade basis — should be < €1/lot and can be attributed to rounding. |

> **Spec agent context:** Futures' distinguishing feature is daily margining — P&L is realized incrementally through variation margin, not at delivery. This changes the P&L reporting model: "realized" has two meanings (margin-realized and delivery-realized). The cascade expiry roll (FUT-VOL-04) is critical and involves closing one position and opening others — must be treated atomically with linked trade references. Peak hour count (FUT-VOL-02) is the most common source of reconciliation breaks with the exchange. Futures are the primary hedging instrument against PPAs — the flat profile of a baseload future vs. the variable profile of a wind PPA creates structural basis risk (the "shape cost" from the PPA section). The system must cross-reference futures positions with PPA exposures for hedge ratio monitoring (PORT-02).

## 7. Exchange / OTC Option

**Types:** European call/put (exercise at expiry only), American call/put (exercise any time).  
**Underlying:** EEX power futures (baseload, peak).  
**Clearing:** ECC (exchange) or bilateral (OTC).  
**Premium:** Paid upfront on trade date.  
**Exercise:** Into the underlying future position.  
**Typical deal count per tenant:** 0–10 active options. Many small-to-mid traders don't use options at all.  
**Position lifecycle:** Execute → pay premium → monitor → exercise decision → position in underlying future → margining → settlement.

### Volume Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OPT-VOL-01 | Notional volume | Same as the underlying future: `lots × lot_size × hours`. The option gives the RIGHT but not the obligation to this volume. The notional is the maximum exposure — actual exposure depends on delta (OPT-VOL-03). The system must track the notional for regulatory position reporting (MiFID II position limits include options on a delta-equivalent basis). |
| OPT-VOL-02 | Exercise volume | Volume materializes only on exercise. European: at expiry. American: at any point before expiry. Un-exercised = zero volume. On exercise, the option is converted into a futures position (or cash-settled for some products). The system must: (a) track whether the option has been exercised, (b) on exercise: create a new futures deal record linked to the option, with execution price = strike price, (c) close the option position, (d) transfer any remaining premium value to the new futures position's cost basis. For European options, ECC auto-exercises if the option is in-the-money (ITM) at expiry by more than a threshold (typically > €0.01/MWh). The system should flag options approaching expiry that are near-ATM and require an explicit exercise/abandon decision. |
| OPT-VOL-03 | Delta-equivalent volume | `notional_volume × delta`. This is the effective volume exposure for position management. Delta ranges 0 to 1 (calls) or -1 to 0 (puts). Changes with underlying price and time to expiry. The delta-equivalent position is what gets aggregated with futures and other positions for net position reporting. A call option with delta 0.5 on 10 MW notional contributes 5 MW to the long position. As the underlying price moves, delta changes (see OPT-VAL-04 gamma) — the system must update the delta-equivalent position whenever delta is recalculated (at minimum daily, ideally on every forward curve update). The position dashboard should show both the notional and delta-equivalent exposure for options. |

### Price Determination

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OPT-PRC-01 | Strike price | Agreed at trade execution. Fixed for the option's lifetime. The strike is the price at which the option holder can buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying future. The distance between the strike and the current forward price determines whether the option is ITM, ATM, or OTM, which drives its value and delta. |
| OPT-PRC-02 | Premium | Price paid by buyer to seller for the option right. Quoted in €/MWh. `total_premium = premium_per_MWh × notional_MWh`. The premium is a sunk cost for the buyer — it's paid regardless of whether the option is exercised. For the seller, the premium is income received in exchange for the obligation to deliver/accept the underlying at the strike price. The premium must be recorded as a separate cashflow (OPT-SET-01) and tracked in the P&L as a cost (for buyers) or income (for sellers). The premium paid at execution is not the same as the option's current market value — the difference is unrealized P&L on the option position. |
| OPT-PRC-03 | Intrinsic value | Call: `max(0, underlying_price − strike)`. Put: `max(0, strike − underlying_price)`. This is the value if exercised immediately. The intrinsic value can be computed at any time using the current forward price for the underlying delivery period. Intrinsic value >= 0 always (options cannot have negative intrinsic value — the holder simply wouldn't exercise). The system must compute intrinsic value daily and display it alongside the option's total value (premium) to show the split between intrinsic and time value. |
| OPT-PRC-04 | Time value | `option_premium − intrinsic_value`. Positive when time remains before expiry. Decays to zero at expiry (theta decay). Time value represents the probability that the option will become (more) profitable before expiry. Deep OTM options have very low intrinsic value but may have material time value if there's enough time and volatility. The time value decay accelerates as expiry approaches (theta increases) — the system should display a time value chart showing the projected decay curve. The time value is what makes options unique compared to forwards/futures — it's the "optionality premium" and it decays with time whether the trader does anything or not. |

### Settlement / Cashflow

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OPT-SET-01 | Premium payment | Paid on trade date or T+1. One-time cashflow from buyer to seller. Regardless of exercise outcome. The premium is the only guaranteed cashflow in an option trade — exercise may or may not occur. The system must: (a) generate a cashflow record for the premium payment on T+0 or T+1 (configurable per exchange), (b) for exchange-traded options: the premium is settled through ECC margin (not a direct bilateral payment), (c) for OTC options: the premium is settled bilaterally per ISDA terms, (d) the premium amount appears as "option premium cost" (buyer) or "option premium income" (seller) in the P&L, separate from the option's MTM. |
| OPT-SET-02 | Exercise settlement | On exercise: option converts to the underlying future at the strike price. Subsequent settlement follows future rules (variation margin etc.). The system must: (a) close the option position (set status to EXERCISED), (b) create a new futures deal with: delivery period = option's underlying, execution price = strike price, volume = option notional, direction = same as option type (call exercise → long future, put exercise → short future), (c) link the new future to the original option for audit trail, (d) begin daily margining on the new futures position from the exercise date, (e) compute the exercise P&L: for a call, `(current_forward − strike) × volume − premium_paid` = total option P&L at exercise. This should reconcile with the cumulative MTM changes tracked during the option's life. |
| OPT-SET-03 | Cash settlement (if applicable) | Some options are cash-settled: `intrinsic_value × notional_volume` paid at expiry. No underlying future position created. Cash-settled options are simpler — no futures position to manage post-exercise. The system must: (a) at expiry, compute the intrinsic value using the official settlement price of the underlying, (b) if intrinsic > 0: generate a cashflow = intrinsic × notional (paid by seller to buyer), (c) close the option position (status = EXPIRED_ITM), (d) if intrinsic = 0: close with status = EXPIRED_OTM, no cashflow. |
| OPT-SET-04 | Initial margin | For short (sold) options: initial margin required. For long (bought) options: premium is the maximum loss, no margin beyond premium paid. The margin asymmetry is important: buying options has defined risk (max loss = premium), but selling options has theoretically unlimited risk (for calls) or risk up to the strike (for puts). The system must correctly model the margin requirement based on whether the option position is long or short. For short options, the IM is calculated by ECC using SPAN and can be substantial — it increases with volatility and as the option moves ITM. |

### Valuation / P&L

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| OPT-VAL-01 | Option MTM (Black-76) | Standard model for European commodity options. Inputs: forward price (F), strike (K), volatility (σ), time to expiry (T), risk-free rate (r). Output: call/put premium. `Call = e^(-rT) × [F × N(d1) − K × N(d2)]` where `d1 = [ln(F/K) + 0.5σ²T] / (σ√T)`, `d2 = d1 − σ√T`. N() = cumulative standard normal distribution. Black-76 is the industry standard for European commodity options — it's an adaptation of Black-Scholes that uses forward prices instead of spot prices. The system must implement Black-76 with high numerical precision (at least 10 decimal places for d1/d2, using a well-tested CDF implementation). Edge cases to handle: (a) T → 0 (at expiry): premium → intrinsic value, (b) σ → 0: premium → discounted intrinsic value, (c) deep ITM: d1 → ∞, N(d1) → 1, premium → (F − K) × e^(-rT), (d) deep OTM: d1 → −∞, N(d1) → 0, premium → 0. The system should validate model outputs against exchange-published option settlement prices (EEX publishes these) as a sanity check. |
| OPT-VAL-02 | Volatility surface | Implied volatility varies by strike (skew) and expiry (term structure). Must maintain a vol surface: σ(K, T). Sourced from exchange (EEX publishes settlement vols) or broker quotes. Interpolation between published strikes/expiries. The volatility surface is a 2D grid: strikes on one axis, expiries on the other, with implied vol at each node. The surface must be smooth (no arbitrage) — the system should interpolate between published nodes using cubic spline or SABR model. Extrapolation beyond the published strike range should use a flat extrapolation or a configurable wing model. The vol surface must be updated daily using the latest exchange-published settlement volatilities. For OTC options where no exchange-published vol exists: broker quotes or an internal vol estimate must be used. The system must track the source of each vol point (exchange, broker, internal estimate) for audit. Volatility is the most sensitive input to option pricing — a 1 percentage point change in vol on a 10 MW Cal option can change the premium by €20K+. |
| OPT-VAL-03 | Greeks — Delta | ∂(option_price)/∂(underlying_price). Used for delta hedging and delta-equivalent position. For calls: `Delta = e^(-rT) × N(d1)`. For puts: `Delta = e^(-rT) × (N(d1) − 1)`. Delta tells the trader: "if the underlying forward moves by €1/MWh, the option value changes by Delta × €1 × volume_MWh." A delta of 0.5 means the option moves half as much as the underlying — to delta-hedge, the trader buys/sells Delta × notional_volume in futures. The system must compute delta and use it for: (a) delta-equivalent position aggregation (OPT-VOL-03), (b) P&L attribution — delta P&L = Delta × change_in_forward × volume, (c) delta hedging recommendations — "to be delta-neutral, sell X MW of the underlying future." |
| OPT-VAL-04 | Greeks — Gamma | ∂²(option_price)/∂(underlying_price)². Rate of change of delta. Highest for ATM options near expiry. Drives hedging rebalancing frequency. `Gamma = e^(-rT) × n(d1) / (F × σ × √T)` where n() = standard normal PDF. Gamma tells the trader: "as the underlying moves, delta changes by Gamma × €1 — so the delta hedge needs rebalancing." High gamma means the hedge becomes stale quickly. Gamma is highest for ATM options near expiry — these require the most active management. The system should alert when gamma exceeds a threshold (configurable per portfolio) indicating the need for frequent hedge rebalancing. Gamma P&L = 0.5 × Gamma × (ΔF)² × volume — the convexity benefit of being long gamma (positive P&L from large moves in either direction). |
| OPT-VAL-05 | Greeks — Vega | ∂(option_price)/∂(volatility). Sensitivity to vol changes. `Vega = e^(-rT) × F × √T × n(d1)`. For power options: high vega = large P&L swings on vol surface updates. Vega tells the trader: "if implied vol changes by 1 percentage point, the option value changes by Vega × volume." Vega is highest for ATM options with long time to expiry — these are the most vol-sensitive. Vega P&L = Vega × Δσ × volume. During periods of market stress, vol can move 5–10 percentage points in a day, causing massive P&L swings for option portfolios. The system should compute vega and include it in the risk dashboard alongside delta exposure. |
| OPT-VAL-06 | Greeks — Theta | ∂(option_price)/∂(time). Daily time decay. Always negative for long options. `Theta = −[e^(-rT) × F × σ × n(d1) / (2√T)] − r × e^(-rT) × [F × N(d1) − K × N(d2)]` (for calls). This is the "cost" of holding the option over time — the system must compute daily theta and display it as: "this option loses €X per day from time decay." Theta accelerates near expiry — the system should project the theta curve and alert when cumulative theta loss is approaching a configurable threshold (e.g., "this option has lost 50% of its original premium to time decay"). Theta P&L = Theta × number_of_days — should be a separate attribution category in the P&L report. |
| OPT-VAL-07 | Greeks — Rho | ∂(option_price)/∂(risk_free_rate). Sensitivity to interest rate changes. Usually immaterial for power options < 1 year. `Rho = K × T × e^(-rT) × N(d2)` (for calls). Rho is included for completeness — for the interest rate environment and typical power option tenors (3–12 months), rho impact is < €100 per 1% rate change for most positions. The system should compute rho but may omit it from the standard P&L report, showing it only in the full Greeks detail view. |
| OPT-VAL-08 | P&L decomposition | Daily P&L = delta P&L (underlying moved) + gamma P&L (convexity) + vega P&L (vol changed) + theta P&L (time decayed) + rho P&L (rates moved). Must reconcile to actual MTM change. The decomposition formula: `ΔP = Delta × ΔF + 0.5 × Gamma × (ΔF)² + Vega × Δσ + Theta × Δt + Rho × Δr + residual`. The residual captures higher-order effects and should be small (< 5% of total P&L change). If the residual is large, it indicates either a model issue or a large overnight move that violates the Taylor expansion assumptions. The system must compute this decomposition daily for each option position and aggregate at portfolio level. This is the most mathematically sophisticated calculation on the platform — it requires consistent Greeks computation across the entire option book. |
| OPT-VAL-09 | Exercise decision | American options: optimal exercise when intrinsic value > holding value (time value ≈ 0). System should flag when exercise is optimal. European: automatic exercise if ITM at expiry (exchange rule). For American options, the system must compute: `holding_value = intrinsic_value + time_value`. If time_value < configurable threshold (e.g., < €0.10/MWh × volume × hours), the system should alert: "early exercise may be optimal — time value has decayed below threshold." The trader makes the final exercise decision, but the system provides the recommendation. For European options at expiry: the system should auto-flag all ITM options and present them for exercise confirmation (or auto-exercise if configured). OTM options at expiry should be auto-expired with notification to the trader. |

> **Spec agent context:** Options are the most mathematically complex instrument. The Black-76 model, volatility surface management, and Greeks are the core implementation challenge. For a CTRM targeting small-to-mid EU traders, options may be Phase 3 — most small shops don't actively trade options. If included, at minimum: MTM using Black-76 (OPT-VAL-01), delta and vega Greeks (OPT-VAL-03, OPT-VAL-05), premium settlement (OPT-SET-01), exercise handling with auto-exercise at expiry (OPT-SET-02, OPT-VAL-09). Full Greeks decomposition (OPT-VAL-08) and gamma/theta/rho (OPT-VAL-04/06/07) can be deferred to a later iteration. The vol surface (OPT-VAL-02) is the hardest data management problem — it requires daily updates from exchange or broker sources, interpolation, and is the most sensitive valuation input. Exercise into futures (OPT-SET-02) requires the system to auto-create a futures deal on exercise, linking it back to the option for audit.

## 8. Cross-Instrument Calculations (Portfolio Level)

These calculations span across all instruments within a portfolio and cannot be attributed to a single instrument type. They depend on the outputs of instrument-level calculations (sections 1–7) and operate on the aggregated portfolio position.

| Req ID | Calculation | Detail | Instruments |
|---|---|---|---|
| PORT-01 | Net position aggregation | Sum all deal-level positions (shaped to common granularity) to produce portfolio CONTRACTED / HEDGED / OPEN view. Requires consistent granularity alignment via shaping engine. The aggregation must handle mixed instrument types: PPA volumes (variable, weather-dependent, at 15-min) + futures (flat MW, shaped from annual/quarterly) + OTC (baseload/peak, shaped) + spot (native 15-min). All must be shaped to a common output granularity for the requested horizon (see cascade tier config). The net position per interval = Σ(BUY volumes) − Σ(SELL volumes). The system must classify each deal's contribution as CONTRACTED (generation/purchase), HEDGED (sell-side hedges), or OPEN (residual). The classification is based on deal direction and deal type, configurable per portfolio. | All |
| PORT-02 | Hedge ratio | `hedged_volume / contracted_volume` per time period. Drives hedging strategy. Track at monthly, quarterly, annual granularity. The hedge ratio tells management: "what percentage of our expected generation is covered by forward sales?" A hedge ratio of 80% means 20% of expected volume is exposed to spot prices. The system must compute hedge ratios at: (a) monthly granularity for the current year (monthly hedging decisions), (b) quarterly for Y+1 and Y+2, (c) annual for Y+3 onward. The hedge ratio should be computed using P50 expected generation as the denominator (not nameplate capacity × hours — that would overstate the unhedged exposure). Hedge ratio targets are set by the risk policy (e.g., "must be > 70% for M+1 through M+6") — the system should display the current ratio against the target and alert on breaches. | PPA (contracted) vs Futures/Swaps/OTC (hedges) |
| PORT-03 | Hedge effectiveness | Correlation between PPA volume/price changes and hedge instrument changes. Required for hedge accounting under IFRS 9. Prospective and retrospective testing. IFRS 9 hedge accounting allows companies to defer P&L on hedge instruments to match the hedged item's P&L timing — but only if the hedge is "effective" (high correlation between the hedge and hedged item). The system must compute: (a) prospective effectiveness at hedge designation: is the expected correlation > 80%? (dollar offset or regression method), (b) retrospective effectiveness at each reporting date: was the actual correlation > 80%? The system must track hedge designations: which specific futures/swap deals are designated as hedges of which specific PPA volumes, with documentation of the hedging strategy and risk being hedged (price risk, volume risk). This is an accounting function — the system should produce the IFRS 9 effectiveness test documentation as a report, not just a number. Phase 1 MVP can defer this to Phase 4. | PPA + hedges |
| PORT-04 | Total P&L reconciliation | Realized + Unrealized across all instruments must reconcile. Cross-check: deal-level P&L sums to portfolio P&L. Daily change decomposes into price/volume/time/new deal/amendment components. The P&L reconciliation is the "truth test" for the entire system. At any point: `Σ(deal_realized_pnl) + Σ(deal_unrealized_pnl) = portfolio_total_pnl`. And: `portfolio_total_pnl[today] − portfolio_total_pnl[yesterday] = Σ(attribution_components)`. If these don't reconcile, there's a bug. The system must run an automated reconciliation check at EOD and flag any breaks > €1. Common causes of reconciliation breaks: (a) missing trades (not imported from exchange), (b) wrong reference price used for MTM, (c) rounding accumulation across many deals, (d) shape profile mismatch between volume and price decomposition, (e) timezone errors in interval alignment. The reconciliation report must show: total P&L, sum of components, residual/break, and the largest contributing deals to the break (for investigation). | All |
| PORT-05 | VaR / PaR / CFaR | Portfolio-level risk metrics. VaR: maximum loss at confidence interval. PaR: P&L distribution. CFaR: cashflow distribution. Historical simulation (min 2 years history) or parametric (variance-covariance). Monte Carlo for non-linear (options). VaR calculation: (a) historical simulation — revalue the portfolio under each of the last 500+ trading days' price changes, sort the P&L outcomes, report the 5th percentile (95% VaR) or 1st percentile (99% VaR). This requires 500+ forward curve histories. (b) Parametric — compute portfolio delta exposure × price volatility × confidence factor (1.65 for 95%, 2.33 for 99%). Simpler but assumes normal distribution (inaccurate for power markets with fat tails). (c) Monte Carlo — simulate 10,000+ price and volume scenarios, revalue portfolio under each, report percentiles. Required if the portfolio contains options (non-linear payoffs). The system should support at minimum parametric VaR for Phase 1, historical simulation for Phase 2, and Monte Carlo for Phase 3 (when options are added). VaR holding period: 1-day (for trading desk risk) and 10-day (for regulatory capital). Reporting frequency: daily EOD. | All |
| PORT-06 | Counterparty exposure netting | Net MTM across all deals per counterparty, respecting netting agreements. Gross exposure vs net exposure. Track against credit limits. The system must maintain: (a) counterparty master data with credit rating, credit limit, netting agreement references, (b) deal-to-netting-group mapping, (c) daily net exposure computation: `net_exposure[CP] = Σ(deal_MTM where MTM > 0, within netting group) − collateral_posted`. Positive net exposure = credit risk (counterparty owes us). Negative = we owe the counterparty (no credit risk to us). The system should provide a counterparty exposure dashboard showing: all counterparties ranked by net exposure, limit utilization %, exposure trend, and drill-down to contributing deals. Pre-trade exposure check: before a new deal is booked, the system should compute the pro-forma exposure including the new deal and alert if it would breach the credit limit. | OTC, PPA, bilateral Swaps |
| PORT-07 | UoM normalization | All volumes normalized to MWh for aggregation. Deals entered in MW (capacity) must be converted using interval hours. Deals in GWh must be scaled. Consistent rounding rules (4 decimal places on MW, 2 on MWh). The system must support volume entry in: MW (most common — represents a capacity per hour), MWh (total energy for a period), GWh (large-scale, typically annual PPA volumes), kW/kWh (small DER). Internal storage and calculation is always in MW and MWh. Conversion: `MWh = MW × hours`, `MW = MWh / hours`, `GWh = MWh / 1000`. Rounding: MW values to 3 decimal places (0.001 MW = 1 kW), MWh values to 2 decimal places (0.01 MWh = 10 kWh). Settlement amounts in EUR to 2 decimal places (cents). The system must not introduce rounding errors through repeated conversions — compute in full precision internally and round only for display and settlement output. | All |
| PORT-08 | Currency normalization | All P&L and MTM in EUR. Non-EUR deals converted at ECB daily fixing rate or contract-specified rate. FX P&L attributed separately from commodity P&L. For Nordic deals (NOK, SEK, DKK): the system must convert to EUR using the ECB reference rate published at 14:15 CET daily. The FX rate used for conversion should be stored with the valuation record for audit. FX P&L = the MTM change attributable to exchange rate movement (holding commodity price constant). Commodity P&L = the MTM change attributable to energy price movement (holding FX rate constant). The sum = total P&L in EUR. For deals with an FX hedge (forward or swap on the currency): the FX hedge P&L should offset the translation P&L — the system should display this netting for FX-hedged deals. | Cross-currency deals |
| PORT-09 | Regulatory position reporting | REMIT standard + non-standard contract reporting. EMIR derivative reporting. MiFID II position limits. Each requires specific data fields — map instrument attributes to regulatory schemas. REMIT reporting (to ACER via registered reporting mechanism): standard contracts within 1 BD of execution, non-standard contracts within 1 month. The system must generate REMIT XML in the prescribed schema, including: UTI (unique transaction identifier), counterparty LEIs, product classification, price, volume, delivery period. EMIR reporting (to trade repositories): derivative contracts (futures, swaps, options) require lifecycle reporting — new, modified, cancelled events. The system must generate EMIR reports and track reporting status per deal. MiFID II position limits: the system must track net positions in commodity derivatives per exchange and product, compare against exchange-published limits, and alert when positions approach limits (typically at 80% of limit). | All (reportable) |
| PORT-10 | Cashflow forecasting | Project future cash inflows/outflows by payment due date across all instruments. Include: settlement amounts, margin calls, premium payments. Used for treasury/liquidity management. The cashflow forecast must aggregate: (a) exchange settlements (DA: D+2, ID: D+1, futures VM: daily), (b) OTC/PPA monthly settlements (M+15 to M+30 BD), (c) expected margin calls (based on current IM requirements + projected VM under stress), (d) option premiums (on trade date), (e) tolerance band / annual true-up settlements (once/year), (f) curtailment compensation (monthly with energy settlement). The forecast should show daily cashflows for the next 30 days, weekly for the next 3 months, and monthly for the next 12 months. The system should also provide a stress cashflow forecast: "if prices move ±20%, what are the worst-case daily cash requirements?" This combines the VM stress with the regular settlement calendar. | All |

## 9. MVP Prioritization Recommendation

| Phase | Instruments | Calculations Included |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 (MVP)** | PPA, OTC Fixed, Futures | All VOL, PRC, SET calculations. Forward MTM. Realized + unrealized P&L. Basic P&L attribution (price/volume). Shape decomposition. Holiday calendar. UoM conversion. Cashflow generation with payment due dates. Capture price and cannibalisation for PPA. Daily variation margin for futures. Cascade expiry roll. Counterparty exposure monitoring. |
| **Phase 2** | + DA, ID, OTC Indexed, Swaps | + Index price resolution. VWAP. Imbalance settlement. Floating leg calculation. Variation margin for cleared swaps. Bilateral netting (EFET). Formula pricing engine. Late payment interest. |
| **Phase 3** | + Options | + Black-76 model. Volatility surface management. Delta and vega Greeks. Premium settlement. Exercise handling (European auto-exercise, American exercise decision support). Delta-equivalent position. |
| **Phase 4** | Portfolio-level risk + regulatory | + Full Greeks (gamma, theta, rho) and P&L decomposition. VaR/PaR/CFaR with Monte Carlo. Hedge effectiveness testing (IFRS 9). CVA. REMIT/EMIR/MiFID II automated reporting. |

> **Rationale:** Phase 1 covers the three instruments that 90% of small-to-mid EU renewable energy traders use daily: PPAs (origination), OTC fixed-price (bilateral hedging), and EEX futures (exchange hedging). The core valuation and settlement engine built for these three extends naturally to the remaining instruments in later phases. DA and ID (Phase 2) are added when the platform supports spot market integration. Options (Phase 3) serve a smaller user base and require significant mathematical infrastructure (Black-76, vol surface). Portfolio risk analytics (Phase 4) are advanced features for more sophisticated trading desks.
